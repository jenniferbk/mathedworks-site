AC2inG: Animated Contrasting Cases in Geometry

  • Grades 6–8
  • Free, no login
  • Internet
  • Unit, Lesson

Twenty-three free animated worked-example pairs for grade 8 geometry, in which students compare two fictional students' solutions, with discussion questions and a teacher guide.

Try one lesson: A.1: Supplementary and Vertical Angles

Erin E. Krupa and 1 other · NSF awards 2019 · latest listed study 2022

Before you use it
  • Free, with no login: the animations run in a web browser at acinggeometry.org, so pairs or small groups need a device and internet. Paper-based versions are posted on the NC State site.
  • These supplement your instruction rather than replace it. Teach the listed prerequisite knowledge first, allow about 15–20 minutes per WEP plus discussion, and use no more than two WEPs a day alongside other instruction and practice.

  • Print the discussion questions: they appear on screen after each pair, but the developers recommend paper copies. The NC State teacher-materials page posts one printable discussion-question PDF per unit.

ASSISTments

  • Item bank, Software

A free web platform where teachers assign math problems from widely used curricula or their own, students get instant feedback and hints, and teachers see reports of common misconceptions.

Try one lesson: Find the Ratio Skill Builder

Neil T. Heffernan · NSF awards 2005–2014 · latest listed study 2023

Before you use it
  • It's free for teachers: sign up for an account. Schools and districts can buy ASSISTments Plus, starting at $8 per student per year with a $1,500 minimum annual contract (checked Oct 25, 2026). CODAP (Common Online Data Analysis Platform)

  • Grades 3–12
  • Free, no login
  • Device per student, Internet
  • Framework or toolkit, Software

A free, browser-based data analysis tool built for students, with drag-and-drop graphs, maps and linked tables, sample datasets and built-in tutorials.

Try one lesson: Roller Coasters

William Finzer and 2 others · NSF awards 2013–2014 · latest listed study 2022

Before you use it
  • Free, with no account or login; it runs in the browser, so nothing needs installing. You need an internet-connected computer and a recent browser; the FAQ says CODAP was developed and tested mainly in Chrome.
  • Phones are too small to use it well, and some features are not yet available on tablets. On an iPad, hold a little longer than expected before dragging.
  • Save your work: saving to Google Drive requires a Google account connected to CODAP; otherwise download documents as .codap files. Only one person at a time can edit a shared document; the FAQ suggests passing it back and forth.
  • Offline use is limited: CODAP works offline only if loaded online first, then accepts only local CSV or TXT data, and embedded web pages, maps and plugins will not load.
  • There are no lessons or assessments in CODAP itself. Plan to use the sample documents or a partner curriculum, or to write your own tasks.

Nine playful activities for high school and college algebra and calculus, mostly online activities, from an NSF-funded research project on mathematical play.

Try one lesson: Graphing Taboo

Amy B Ellis and 1 other · NSF awards 2024 · latest listed study 2025

Before you use it
  • The site posts no price and asks for no sign-up to read the activity pages, and the Graphing Taboo PDF downloaded without a login. The other eight activities open on Amplify Classroom (the older teacher.desmos.com links redirect there); the site doesn't say whether you need an Amplify account to run them with a class.
  • You'll need internet-connected devices for the eight online activities; the site doesn't say whether students work one per device or in pairs. Graphing Taboo needs printed card sets, one set per student in groups of four.
  • No teacher notes, grade levels, timings or standards are posted on the activity pages, so work through an activity yourself before class. Drone Delivery says students should complete Go Kart first.
  • The project is aimed at high school and undergraduate algebra and calculus. Several activities are calculus ideas (Pool Fill uses Riemann sums; Major Tom links acceleration to velocity and displacement, and Drone Delivery to velocity and position), so check fit for your course.
  • These are designs from a research project that runs to July 2028, and the activity titles carry version labels (Major Tom v13, Go Kart v11), so activities may change or move.

Spanish editions, planned in the NSF proposal, aren't mentioned by the publisher.

A 40-hour geometry professional development course for grades 5–10 teachers, with 20 two-hour sessions: 11 doing math together and 9 analyzing student work.

Mark Driscoll · NSF awards 2004–2008 · latest listed study 2008

Before you use it
  • Only institutions can buy it: Heinemann marks it a restricted product, sold to buyers ordering for an educational institution, so an individual teacher can't order it alone. It lists at $351.33, or $263.50 at the web/school price (checked Oct 25, 2026).
  • This is PD for a facilitator to run with a group of six to twenty teachers of grades 5–10, not classroom lessons. You don't need to be a geometry expert, but the authors look for some experience leading study groups or professional development, or a willingness to learn.
  • Plan the year: ten four-hour monthly meetings or twenty two-hour biweekly ones. Schedule each Analyze Student Work session at least two weeks after its Do Math session so teachers can try the problem with their students.
  • You'll need equipment to show video and a computer that reads the DVD-ROM of handouts, videos and applets. Necessary supplies include patty paper, string, straws, straightedges, scissors and sticky notes in four colors; Geometer's Sketchpad and computer access are listed as very helpful, not required.
  • Published in 2008.

Transition to College Mathematics and Statistics (TCMS)

  • Free, no login
  • Specific software
  • Full curriculum, Teacher guide

A free fourth-year high-school course in statistics, functions, financial math, counting, informatics and voting, for college-bound students who won't take calculus.

Try one lesson: Unit 1, Lesson 1, Investigation 1: Summarizing and Displaying the Risk

Christian R. Hirsch · NSF awards 2010

Before you use it
  • The materials are free: the authors permit free public use, and commercial use needs their permission. The files were ported from the last published edition, and the authors warn of possible typos and design glitches.
  • The teacher's guide points to McGraw-Hill's ConnectED for the Unit Resource Masters, which hold assessments and the College Readiness Assessment sets. We found lesson quizzes inside the free teacher's guide file but did not check whether every resource master is there.
  • Technology is assumed: the course uses TCMS-Tools (Java-based) and graphing calculators. The TCMS-Tools address printed in the guide returns 404 (checked Oct 25, 2026), so plan on other spreadsheet, CAS, statistics and geometry software.
  • Students need a full algebra-geometry-algebra II sequence or three years of integrated mathematics first. Lessons run long: in Unit 1, Lessons 1–3 are paced at 9, 10 and 8 days, including assessment, and the Looking Back lesson at 2 days. The guide advises keeping the units in textbook order, since review tasks are designed for it.
  • The guide cites the Common Core State Standards and NCTM's Principles to Actions (2014); no publication date appears in the text we saved.

Video in the Middle (VIM)

  • Grades 6–8
  • Free, no login
  • Internet
  • Unit, PD materials

Forty free online modules on teaching middle-school algebra, each built around a classroom video clip, a math task and journal reflections.

Try one lesson: Discussing 5 by 5: Framing a Pattern Task

Nanette Seago and 3 others · NSF awards 1998–2017 · latest listed study 2023

Before you use it
  • Free to view: the module we checked opened on WestEd's public Canvas site without logging in (checked Oct 25, 2026). WestEd also offers Common Cartridge versions for a district LMS and customized professional learning facilitated by WestEd staff; the site does not say what these cost.
  • The Canvas course we checked (VIM 10) says its content is offered under a Public Domain license unless otherwise noted; other modules' notices were not checked.
  • You need an internet connection and a device that plays streamed video. In the module we checked, the clip is on Vimeo and the journal is a separate download. Canvas and Vimeo are third-party hosts, so links can change.
  • Plan about two hours per module, plus time to try ideas in your own classroom. Print the task before you start: the first step asks you to download and print it, solve it, and photograph your work for the journal.
  • The content is middle-school algebra: patterns, linear functions, expressions and equations, with a similarity task cluster. The modules cite NCTM's Principles to Actions (2014) teaching practices. WestEd lists the collection with a 2023 copyright. The VIM project draws on the older Learning and Teaching Linear Functions video-case materials.

An online archive of 300+ research videos of students from grade 1 through high school reasoning about mathematics, with transcripts, for studying student thinking.

Try one lesson: Building large models to show equivalence, an exploration, Clip 1 of 4: Is there a larger model?

Carolyn A. Maher and 2 others · NSF awards 2007–2012 · latest listed study 2016

Before you use it
  • The audience is teacher educators, researchers and teachers studying student thinking: the videos show researchers working with students, not ready-made lessons for students.
  • Viewing needs no account: on Oct 25, 2026 we searched the collection, and a clip's player (13 minutes, 32 seconds) and its transcript opened without logging in. Creating analytics requires joining the VMC Community by application, limited to people in education research, training and practice, and agreeing to a code of conduct.
  • The code of conduct says videos are for streaming only and may not be downloaded, or used commercially, without written permission from the collection owners, even though search results label the video links "Download MP4".
  • Footage dates from 1992 onward. The 2007 award that proposed the database planned to index episodes to NCTM's process standards for reasoning and justification.
  • Videos come with transcripts; time per video and suggested uses beyond the FAQ's general advice are not stated.
