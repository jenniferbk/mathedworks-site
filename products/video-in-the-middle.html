Video in the Middle (VIM)

Forty free online modules on teaching middle-school algebra, each built around a classroom video clip, a math task and journal reflections.

  • Grades 6–8
  • Free, no login
  • Internet
  • Unit, PD materials

Get the materialsvideointhemiddle.org

Before you use it

  • Free to view: the module we checked opened on WestEd's public Canvas site without logging in (checked Oct 25, 2026). WestEd also offers Common Cartridge versions for a district LMS and customized professional learning facilitated by WestEd staff; the site does not say what these cost.
  • The Canvas course we checked (VIM 10) says its content is offered under a Public Domain license unless otherwise noted; other modules' notices were not checked.
  • You need an internet connection and a device that plays streamed video. In the module we checked, the clip is on Vimeo and the journal is a separate download. Canvas and Vimeo are third-party hosts, so links can change.
  • Plan about two hours per module, plus time to try ideas in your own classroom. Print the task before you start: the first step asks you to download and print it, solve it, and photograph your work for the journal.
  • The content is middle-school algebra: patterns, linear functions, expressions and equations, with a similarity task cluster. The modules cite NCTM's Principles to Actions (2014) teaching practices. WestEd lists the collection with a 2023 copyright. The VIM project draws on the older Learning and Teaching Linear Functions video-case materials.

What a teacher gets

Video in the Middle is a set of forty online professional development modules from WestEd for teachers of middle-school algebra. Each module takes about two hours plus classroom application and is built around a rich task and a video clip from a real classroom. The modules follow a common pattern: try the task, compare solution methods, review the lesson context, analyze the clip, and apply it to practice through reflection. Modules can be found by task cluster (pattern or similarity tasks), mathematics topic, or NCTM's Principles to Actions teaching practices, and combined into "playlists."

We worked through the structure of one module, Discussing 5 by 5 (the Pool Border task). In its eleven steps on a public Canvas course, you print the task, solve it and photograph your work, record reflections in a downloadable module journal, watch the clip, examine a transcript, re-watch the clip with mathematics educator comments, read a Bridge to Practice vignette from another classroom, and close with a journal reflection. Other modules were not checked. Individual teachers, district PD leaders and teacher educators can use them.

How these awards connect to this product

The VIM site credits NSF support through awards 9731339 and 1720507. 1720507 (WestEd, PI Seago) is the award whose abstract describes the forty online video-based modules on middle-school algebra; it funded this product. 9731339 (San Diego State University Foundation, PI Branca, co-PIs Mumme and Seago) funded video-case professional development materials; its abstract names different proposed modules (Algebraic Thinking as Generalized Arithmetic, Algebraic Thinking from a Geometry Perspective, Modeling, and Language and Representation) and not Learning and Teaching Linear Functions. It is listed only because the VIM site credits it; its tie to the materials VIM draws on is inferred.
