ASSISTments

A free web platform where teachers assign math problems from widely used curricula or their own, students get instant feedback and hints, and teachers see reports of common misconceptions.

  • Grades 3–12
  • Free with registration
  • Device per student, Internet
  • Item bank, Software

Get the materialsassistments.org

Before you use it

  • It's free for teachers: sign up for an account. Schools and districts can buy ASSISTments Plus, starting at $8 per student per year with a $1,500 minimum annual contract (checked Oct 25, 2026). Check whether your district needs a signed data privacy or service agreement before students log in; ASSISTments' FAQ lists a question about getting one completed.
  • You'll need an internet-connected device for each student. It runs in the browser on Chromebooks, laptops and tablets with no special software.
  • Content runs from grade 1 through high school, but because students mostly use it for independent practice, ASSISTments says it works best with students in grade 4 and above.
  • ASSISTments' content is aligned to several widely used curricula (Illustrative Mathematics, Open Up Resources, Eureka Math/EngageNY) and other standards-aligned resources, plus its own Skill Builders. You can also use your own curriculum and write your own problems.
  • ASSISTments says it uses AI to support students, and teachers can get AI summaries of class misconceptions. ASSISTments says teachers stay in control and are told when AI is involved.

Try one lesson

Find the Ratio Skill Builder app.assistments.org

  • Grade 6
  • Find the Ratio (6.RP.A.1)
  • Time not stated
  • Free, no login

Students find ratios in reduced form from word problems, such as the ratio of pets to dogs when a pet shop has 660 cats and 440 dogs, typing answers like 3:4 and getting immediate feedback. In a Skill Builder they keep answering until they get three in a row correct.

You need: A device with a web browser for each student.

Anyone can view the problems without logging in; assigning them to students is done from a free ASSISTments teacher account.

What a teacher gets

ASSISTments is a free online math platform run by The ASSISTments Foundation, a nonprofit started by its developers at Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI). Teachers assign problem sets; students get immediate feedback, hints or explanations as they work; and teachers see reports of student responses, common misconceptions and overall performance. The developers say it serves grades 1–12.

The content library holds more than 200,000 problems. Teachers can find and assign student-facing materials from Illustrative Mathematics (Kendall Hunt IM and IM v.360), Open Up Resources and Eureka Math/EngageNY, such as practice problems, cool-downs and unit assessments, along with ASSISTments' own Skill Builders and released state practice tests. They can also write their own problems or combine existing ones, and assign in practice (formative) or test (summative) mode. Suggested uses include homework, independent practice, exit tickets, small-group instruction, intervention and test preparation. ASSISTments integrates with Google Classroom, Canvas, Schoology and Clever.

How these awards connect to this product

NSF funded part of ASSISTments. The 2005 CAREER award (0448319) describes creating the web-based tutoring system and its "assistments"; 1109483 builds and tests new functionality on ASSISTments; 1440753 adds research accounts to the platform. ASSISTments dates its start to 2003, before these awards, and says Heffernan has received federal and philanthropic funding for it; no source ties today's platform, or any specific feature of it, to a particular award. Awards whose records only use or build on the platform for research (1252297, 1724889, 1931523) and shortlisted awards whose records do not tie them to the math platform (0733286, 1535428, 1759229) are not listed.
