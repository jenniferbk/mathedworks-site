IAS/PCMI—The Teacher Program Series (AMS books)

Eight AMS books of problem sets from the Park City Mathematics Institute's summer teacher program, for middle and high school teachers' own mathematics learning.

  • Grades 6–12
  • Paid
  • No devices needed
  • Unit, PD materials

Get the materialsbookstore.ams.org

Before you use it

  • These are paid books from the AMS bookstore, priced per volume. Softcover list prices: $45 (volumes 1, 2 and 4–7), $35 (volume 3), $50 (volume 8). eBooks are $35 for volumes 1–7 and $50 for volume 8. AMS also sells print-plus-eBook bundles, and MAA and AMS members pay less.
  • AMS says volumes 1–7 are designed for precollege teachers. It lists the readership of volumes 1–3 as in-service and future secondary school teachers, volumes 4–7 as teachers of middle and high school mathematics, and volume 8 as undergraduate students interested in professional development for middle and high school teachers; volume 8's facilitator notes are for using the book with students at any level.
  • The volume 2 preface says the summer course behind the books meets for 15 two-hour sessions. If you want to lead a group, every volume lists facilitator notes or a facilitator guide and solutions in its table of contents.
  • Topics range from probabilistic number theory and permutation groups to Penrose tilings and Voronoi diagrams in two and three dimensions. AMS says volume 7's topics connect to precollege mathematics as core ideas or as enrichment for standard geometry topics, so pick the volume that fits your teaching.
  • The volumes date from 2015 to 2021. The volume 2 preface ties the course design to the Common Core Standards for Mathematical Practice.

Problem Set 1 (Applications of Algebra and Geometry to the Work of Teaching, preview) ams.org

  • Segment lengths on square and isometric dot paper
  • Time not stated

For teachers: find which segment lengths are possible on 6-by-6 square, then isometric, dot paper; conjecture about larger grids and which numbers can be distances. Tough Stuff: find a non-right integer-sided triangle with no horizontal or vertical segments on square dot paper, and a scalene integer-sided triangle on isometric dot paper, or prove none exists.

You need: Square and isometric dot paper (the problem set says one copy of each is included)

This is AMS's preview of volume 2, linked from its bookstore page; the PDF opened without a login, and no price is stated for it. It is teacher-level mathematics from a PD course, not a student lesson.

What a teacher gets

These books come from Park City Mathematics Institute summer teacher courses. AMS describes volumes 1–7 as each covering one summer's course, independent of the rest, and as not a course in the traditional sense but a carefully sequenced collection of problem sets; for volumes 1–5 it adds that readers uncover the themes for themselves. AMS describes volume 8 as a full course on continued fractions in 14 problem sets, with solutions and facilitator notes. Every volume's table of contents lists facilitator notes (a facilitator guide in volume 4) and solutions (for selected problems in volume 8); volumes 1–3 add teaching notes and a mathematical overview. In the volume 2 sample, problem sets are split into Important Stuff, Neat Stuff and Tough Stuff.

The eight volumes are Probability through Algebra, Applications of Algebra and Geometry to the Work of Teaching, Famous Functions in Number Theory, Some Applications of Geometric Thinking, Moving Things Around, Probability and Games, Fractions, Tilings, and Geometry and Fractions: To Be Continued. AMS lists Bowen Kerins, Al Cuoco and Glenn Stevens as authors of every volume.

How these awards connect to this product

NSF award 0554309 (Mathematics for Teaching: A Problem-based Resource for Teachers, Institute for Advanced Study; Griffiths PI, Cuoco and Clemens co-PIs; 2006–2013) set out to "produce, publish and disseminate" a mathematics content curriculum for in-service secondary teachers from the PCMI summer program. The part of its project outcomes report that NSF's API returns (the text is cut off mid-sentence) says the project "polished into a published set of professional development materials" the PCMI problem-solving courses and that "The American Mathematics Society is the publisher of the volumes in the Mathematics for Teaching series," and that part names three titles: Famous Functions in Number Theory, Probability through Algebra and Applications of Algebra and Geometry (as "...to the Craft of Teaching"). Those match AMS volumes 3, 1 and 2 of IAS/PCMI—The Teacher Program Series (all 2015; volume 2 is titled "...to the Work of Teaching"). The NSF record names no later volume, volumes 4–8 appeared in 2016–2021 after the award ended, and volume 8 is based on a 2018 course, so we use "Funded part of this product." The prefaces of volumes 1–4 that we read do not mention NSF. We also checked 9653447 (IAS/PCMI Faculty Enhancement Program, 1997–2000): it funded undergraduate faculty at PCMI and does not connect to these books.
