Before you use it

  • The materials are free: the authors permit free public use, and commercial use needs their permission. The files were ported from the last published edition, the CCSS Edition (the Course 1 teacher's guide is dated 2015 and refers to SBAC and PARCC assessments), and the authors warn of possible typos and design glitches; earlier editions were also sold as Contemporary Mathematics in Context.
  • The teacher's guides still point to McGraw-Hill's ConnectED for the Unit Resource Masters and online assessment. The free release is the student and teacher files plus three guides; we found lesson quizzes and unit tests inside the Course 1 teacher's guide but did not check every resource master.
  • Plan on technology: graphing calculators with iteration are assumed for class work and homework, CAS is desirable, and some homework expects computer access. The Java-based CPMP-Tools software the guide relies on is no longer at the address it prints (404, checked Oct 25, 2026); NCTM's Core Math Tools page also returns 404, and the old wmich.edu/cpmp project address now redirects to an unrelated commercial blog.
  • Course 1 needs classroom supplies such as rubber bands and weights, meter sticks, compasses, protractors, dice, linkage strips, mirror tiles, pipe cleaners and a penny per student; each unit planning guide lists what that unit uses.
  • Plan for a shift in teaching and for time: the developers recommend starting with Course 1 and adding a course each year, and that first-time teachers work at least in pairs with a common planning period. Course 1 planning guides allot, for example, 13 days for one statistics lesson including assessment, and some Course 1 lessons are marked optional depending on students' middle school background.

Working in teams, students simulate a bungee jump, recording the stretched length of a cord with various weights attached and looking for the pattern in a table and graph. Then they analyze data on how the price of a jump at the amusement park affects the number of daily customers.

You need: Rubber bands, fishing weights, bags of nuts and bolts or other weights, and meter sticks.

What a teacher gets

Core-Plus Mathematics is a problem-based, inquiry-oriented high-school program that replaces the algebra-geometry-algebra II-precalculus sequence with integrated courses. Each year weaves together algebra and functions, geometry and trigonometry, statistics and probability, and discrete mathematics. Courses 1-3 are a common core intended for all students; Course 4: Preparation for Calculus serves STEM-bound students, and a separate fourth-year course, Transition to College Mathematics and Statistics, has its own card. The authors now post student and teacher editions of all five courses as PDF and Word files, plus an implementation guide.

Each lesson has 2-5 investigations run as a four-phase cycle: a teacher-led launch, small-group investigation (groups of two to four), a whole-class Summarize the Mathematics discussion, and an individual Check Your Understanding. On Your Own homework sets mix applications, connections, reflections, extensions and review tasks, and each unit closes with a Looking Back lesson. Course 1 has eight units, from Patterns of Change to Patterns in Chance. Unit planning guides give objectives, assignments, materials and pacing in days, and the Course 1 teacher's guide file includes lesson quizzes and unit tests.

How these awards connect to this product

The authors say the project's research and development was supported by a series of NSF grants to Western Michigan University, with Hirsch as director, and that CPMP was first funded in 1992; neither the website nor the guides list award numbers. The three awards listed are Hirsch's Instructional Materials Development awards whose NSF records describe the curriculum itself - 9255257 (the original three-year curriculum), 9618193 (fourth-year materials) and 0137718 (a revision of student and teacher materials). The free files are ported from the later CCSS Edition, and no source says which award funded that edition. Hirsch's 9353513 (a summer master's program for teachers) and 1201917 (Core Math Tools software) were left off because their records do not describe the curriculum; 1020312 is listed on the separate TCMS card. The NSF awards listed ended by 2009; the free files are the 2015 CCSS Edition, and no source says NSF funded that edition, so the relation is recorded as an earlier edition.
