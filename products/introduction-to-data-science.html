Introduction to Data Science (IDS)

A high-school data science course in which students collect their own data with phones and analyze it in R/RStudio; offered to schools through a paid UCLA partnership.

  • Grades 9–12
  • Agreement or training required
  • Device per student, Internet, Specific software

Get the materialsidsucla.org

Before you use it

  • Classroom use requires a district or school partnership with UCLA IDS. The site says implementation needs training and technical support, offered only to partners on a fee-for-service basis, and the IDS team cannot support teachers outside a partnership.
  • Costs, per a partnership cost sheet linked from the site: $2,925 per participant for 3 required PD days (6 more days optional at $1,575), and technology at $72 per student and $204 per teacher per year. The sheet states no period, and an asterisked note says grant funding for no-cost PD for 2027-2028 and beyond had ended. The FAQ describes a different PD load (9 days in year 1, 4 in year 2) and still advertises a January 2025 webinar, so confirm current pricing with IDS.
  • Districts apply after an informational webinar the site says is held each January; the page we captured still advertises the 2024-2025 webinar.
  • Technology: students need wifi-enabled mobile devices and/or internet-connected computers to collect data and code in R/RStudio. The partnership page offers a limited 2-week trial of the technology through a sandbox.
  • The PD has teachers work through the curriculum as learners first; the site notes that teachers most likely have not been taught these skills in their own undergraduate education.

Try one lesson

Lesson 2: Stick Figures curriculum.idsucla.org

  • High school
  • Observe, record, and organize data
  • Time not stated
  • Free, no login

Acting as researchers, student teams record everything they can from 8 stick-figure cards, organize the data on a poster any way they choose, give feedback in a gallery walk, and discuss which representations make comparisons easy, leading to the idea of variables.

You need: Printed Stick Figures cards cut into sets of 8 (one set per team), poster paper, markers and sticky notes.

The lesson opens by discussing the Data Diary homework from Lesson 1. It comes from the version 6.2 curriculum posted for LAUSD under a CC BY-SA license.

What a teacher gets

Introduction to Data Science is a high-school course from UCLA's Mobilize project that teaches statistics and data analysis alongside coding in R through RStudio. It has four units: Data and Visualizations; Distributions, Probability, and Simulations; Data Collection Methods; and Predictions and Models. Students run "participatory sensing" campaigns, collecting data about their own lives (snacks, time use, stress) on a mobile app, then analyze it. The site says IDS addresses the Common Core high-school Statistics and Probability standards and modeling, and that it is a University of California "c"-approved mathematics course.

The public table of contents (version 3.0) lists, for each unit, sections of lessons paired with RStudio labs, practicum tasks, and an end-of-unit project. We read the table of contents, the topic outline and one campaign guideline; the full curriculum is offered for download through an interest form, which we did not complete.

How these awards connect to this product

The IDS site lists "The National Science Foundation–MOBILIZE Grant" among its funders. NSF award 0962919, "MOBILIZE: Mobilizing for Innovative Computer Science Teaching and Learning" (UCLA, PI Gould), is a Math and Science Partnership with LAUSD that developed participatory-sensing curricula for high-school mathematics and science; the IDS site says IDS is one of four Mobilize curricula. IDS has also had California Department of Education and College Futures Foundation funding. Relation set to 'Funded this product' on the strength of the site's funder list and its naming IDS as a Mobilize curriculum; the award abstract itself does not name IDS.
