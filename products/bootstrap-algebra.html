Bootstrap:Algebra

A free algebra module for grades 7–12 in which students program a video game in Pyret while working with functions, linear relationships, inequalities and the distance formula.

  • Grades 6–12
  • Free, no login
  • Device per student, Internet
  • Unit, Curriculum

Get the materialsbootstrapworld.org

Before you use it

  • Free with no login: lesson plans, slides and the 136-page student workbook PDF are on the website; printed workbooks can be bought from Lulu. Answer keys are teacher-only and need a free Bootstrap account, which takes a day or two to be approved.
  • Students need computers that can reach pyret.BootstrapWorld.org; in the Simple Data Types lesson they program in Driver/Navigator pairs. Workbook pages can be done without a computer. Whether students must sign in (for example with Google) to save programs isn't stated.
  • License: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. You can use and share the materials for non-commercial teaching but not share modified versions, and running training or professional development with them needs written approval.
  • Time per lesson isn't stated. Optional parts use outside tools; for example, the linear-functions lesson's Desmos activity has to be shared with students and linked into the slide deck.
  • Current: the Fall 2026 materials were updated September 24, 2026. An older WeScheme version and an archive of earlier years are also available.

What a teacher gets

Bootstrap:Algebra is meant to be taught inside a regular math class by teachers with no programming experience, or as a standalone computer science class. Students write programs in Pyret, a text-based language, in a browser editor. The Fall 2026 Pyret edition has 27 lessons, from data types and order of operations (using "Circles of Evaluation") through function notation, linear functions, word problems with the "Design Recipe," compound inequalities, piecewise functions and the distance formula. Lessons marked for the project build toward a video game with a player, a danger and a target; flag, logo and function projects are alternatives.

The lesson plan we read on linear functions lists goals, preparation and key points for the teacher, then moves through launch, investigate and synthesize phases with Notice and Wonder pages and an optional Desmos activity. Every activity has a page in a student workbook, a free 136-page PDF. Google Slides, a contracts reference, a glossary (also bilingual English–Spanish) and a standards alignment are provided.

How these awards connect to this product

The Bootstrap:Algebra curriculum page credits NSF awards 1042210, 1535276, 1648684 and 1738598. 1042210 (Fisler, WPI) is a broadening-participation award proposing Bootstrap as a computing curriculum built on algebra and coordinate geometry; 1535276 (Krishnamurthi, Brown) studies transfer from Bootstrap to algebra; 1648684 (Krishnamurthi, Brown) builds tools to make text and block programming accessible to visually impaired users; 1738598 (Fisler, Brown) develops professional development for teachers using Bootstrap. The Pyret course page also credits 2031479 and 1501927; in the NSF API both are Education Development Center awards on data-science and big-data career pathways that do not mention Bootstrap, so they are not listed. No source says which award funded which lessons; the records for 1648684 (accessibility tooling) and 1738598 (teacher PD) do not describe developing lesson content.
