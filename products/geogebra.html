Before you use it

  • Free for you and your students in school. GeoGebra's license covers non-commercial use; commercial users pay a license fee.
  • Use it in a browser or download the apps for any device. GeoGebra suggests the Calculator Suite for most teachers and students, and offers exam modes if your tests restrict internet access.
  • The million-plus community resources are written by users, and quality varies. An independent analysis of 347 community applets on function transformations found that most hid the transformation's defining parameters and only a few included questions or tasks to guide students. The GeoGebra Content Team's resources are sorted by grade.
  • A free account lets you save your progress and customize and share resources.

What a teacher gets

GeoGebra is dynamic mathematics software that brings geometry, algebra, spreadsheets, graphing, statistics and calculus together in one engine. It runs in the browser and as apps for all devices; GeoGebra recommends its Calculator Suite for most teachers and students, and also offers separate graphing, geometry, 3D, CAS and scientific calculators, plus exam modes for paper-based tests.

Beyond the apps, GeoGebra hosts free resources. Its staff content team has written ready-to-use activities for grades 4–8 and high school in algebra, geometry, number sense, measurement, operations, and statistics and probability, sorted by grade band. Users worldwide have posted over one million more activities, exercises and lessons. GeoGebra Classroom lets you share an activity with students and monitor their progress in real time. Three peer-reviewed studies by university researchers with no tie to GeoGebra we could find look at GeoGebra in a classroom, with teachers in a professional development program, and in its applet library (listed below).

Who made it

GeoGebra, developed by GeoGebra GmbH (Linz, Austria); part of the BYJU'S group since 2021. Developed by GeoGebra GmbH.

Research behind it

  1. GeoGebra for learning and teaching: A parallel investigationMthembeni Mthethwa, Anass Bayaga (University of Zululand), Michael J. Bossé (Appalachian State University), Derek Williams (Montana State University). South African Journal of Education, 40(2), Art., 2020. Student outcomes.Grade 11 learners (N = 56) in a high-poverty, rural South African high school, half taught circle geometry with GeoGebra activities and half by lecture; the authors report that the GeoGebra group was more successful at solving problems and justifying their statements. A parallel strand looked at one teacher's beliefs.SAJE editorial policies: "All articles will be submitted to referees (national and/or international)." ERIC lists it as peer reviewed. Authors at the University of Zululand, Appalachian State University and Montana State University. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The authors and universities don't appear on GeoGebra's archived team or institutes pages.
  2. Enacting Core Practices of Effective Mathematics Pedagogy with GeoGebraIsaac Benning (University of Cape Coast, Ghana). Mathematics Teacher Education and Development, 23(2), pp. 101–127, 2021. Teacher perceptions or use.Study of teachers' use of GeoGebra inside a professional development program: eleven Ghanaian in-service teachers from one senior high school spent twelve months in PD building GeoGebra-based lessons and teaching them to peers and then students. The author identifies 31 core practices across five themes and reports that most teachers found it hard to engage students in deep mathematical discussion.MTED, a refereed journal of MERGA, uses double-blind review: "The manuscript is reviewed blind by at least two referees, one of which is a member of the Editorial Board." Single author at the University of Cape Coast. Funding statement names the University of Otago Doctoral Scholarship; no conflict-of-interest statement. The author and university don't appear on GeoGebra's archived team or institutes pages.
  3. Opportunity to Learn Function Transformations in Dynamic Mathematical Environment: An Analysis of 347 GeoGebra AppletsXiangquan Yao, Nicholas Grande (Pennsylvania State University). Proceedings of the 45th Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), Vol. 2, pp. 326–335, 2023. Curriculum analysis.Coded 347 unique applets on function transformations from the GeoGebra website. Most applets did not show the transformations' defining parameters or corresponding points, and only a small number included questions or tasks to scaffold students' exploration.Proceedings preface gives acceptance rates (research reports 45%, brief research reports 70%, posters 90%); it does not describe the review process itself. ERIC lists the paper as peer reviewed. Both authors at Pennsylvania State University. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The authors and university don't appear on GeoGebra's archived team or institutes pages.

Each study is about this product, by researchers with no tie to its developer we could find.

Who funded it

NSF did not fund this product. It is listed because independent researchers studied it in peer-reviewed venues (see Research behind it). GeoGebra GmbH in Linz, Austria develops GeoGebra; in 2021 it became part of the BYJU'S group, where it operates as an independent unit under its original founders and developers. Commercial users pay a license fee, which GeoGebra says helps keep it free for non-commercial users. No saved source names any other funder.
