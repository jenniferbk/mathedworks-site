Illustrative Mathematics 6–12 Math (IM 6–8 Math and IM 9–12 Math)

A free, openly licensed, problem-based curriculum for grades 6–8 and high school (Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2 or Integrated Math 1–3), with full teacher lesson plans at AccessIM.org.

  • Grades 6–12
  • Free with registration
  • Internet
  • Full curriculum, Teacher guide

Get the materialsaccessim.org

Before you use it

  • Lessons open at AccessIM.org without an account, but assessments, cool-downs and sample student responses need a free educator account. To register you upload a government-issued photo ID and take a live photo, or get an invitation from a verified colleague at your school.
  • Plan on prep: some activities use card sets you copy and cut ahead of time (Grade 7 Lesson 1 gives each pair 10 slips cut from a blackline master), and digital versions of activities need devices that run the applets. Lesson slides aren't on AccessIM; Kendall Hunt offers them separately and sells printed workbooks and teacher guides.
  • Lessons are designed for 45 minutes, with practice problems outside that time.
  • The free site now carries IM v.360 (2024), a revision of the earlier courses; the studies listed here looked at earlier versions (Grade 7 and Algebra 1 from 2019, Geometry as cited in 2023). The prior version is still free at im.kendallhunt.com.

Try one lesson

Unit 1, Lesson 1: What Are Scaled Copies? accessim.org

  • Grade 7
  • Scaled copies
  • 45 minutes (IM's design for 6–12 lessons); the page lists 10, 10 and 15 minutes for its three activities
  • Free, no login

Students compare five copies of a portrait to decide which are scaled copies, then identify which drawings of the letter F are scaled copies by comparing lengths and draw their own. In pairs, they take turns matching polygon cards that are scaled copies and explaining how they know.

You need: Chart paper, sticky notes, the 6–12 Blank Math Community Chart, and the Pairs of Scaled Polygons cards cut from the blackline master; devices if you use the digital applet versions.

The lesson page opens without an account; the cool-down and sample student responses need a verified educator account. IM v.360 is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 (noncommercial use with attribution).

What a teacher gets

Illustrative Mathematics (IM) is a nonprofit that writes a problem-based math curriculum and posts it free at AccessIM.org. This card covers the middle and high school courses: Grades 6, 7 and 8 (plus accelerated 6 and 7), and Algebra 1, Geometry and Algebra 2 or Integrated Math 1–3. The 6–8 courses began as Open Up Resources 6–8 Math, which IM authored; Open Up Resources still offers its version in print and digital formats.

Lessons in grades 6–12 are designed for 45 minutes. The Grade 7 lesson we checked has a warm-up, two activities and a lesson synthesis, with a cool-down to check understanding; its activities give the student task, launch directions and notes on likely student thinking, and two of them have print and digital (applet) versions. Assessments include pre-unit diagnostics, practice problems, section checkpoints, and mid- and end-of-unit assessments. Spanish student materials are available for grades 6–8, accelerated 6–7, Algebra 1 and Integrated Math 1. Four peer-reviewed PME-NA papers by university researchers analyze IM units or teachers' views of IM (listed below).

Who made it

Illustrative Mathematics (IM); the 6–8 courses are also sold as Open Up Resources 6–8 Math. Developed by Bill McCallum and Kristin Umland.

Research behind it

  1. Identifying Opportunities to Engage in Literacy Practices: A Framework for Analyzing Curriculum MaterialsEthan Smith (University of Delaware). Proceedings of the 42nd Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), pp. 400–408, 2020. Curriculum analysis.Applies a framework for literacy opportunities (reading, writing, speaking, listening) to the first six lessons of IM Grade 7 Unit 2, Introducing Proportional Relationships (2019 version). The author reports that the framework shows how literacy opportunities are structured and connected within tasks, and how many remain unstructured.Proceedings front matter: all submissions "were strictly peer-reviewed in a double-blind process by three (sometimes four) colleagues." Single author at the University of Delaware. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The author and university don't appear on IM's team, partners or research pages.
  2. How Algebra Teachers' Perceptions of the Illustrative Mathematics Curriculum Evolved over Three YearsMollie H. Appelgate, Ji Yeong I, Anne Foegen, Betsy Araujo Grando (all Iowa State University). Proceedings of the 44th Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), pp. 247–248 (poster), 2022. Teacher perceptions or use.Interviews with Algebra 1 teachers in one urban district in year 1 (2019–20, 22 teachers) and year 3 (2021–22, 13 teachers) of using IM. In both years more than half noted the benefit of IM's real-world contexts and a challenge of not enough time; year 1 teachers valued ready-to-use materials, year 3 teachers more often the curriculum's design, such as how it spiraled the content.Proceedings front matter: "Each paper was reviewed by multiple referees in a double-blind process"; overall acceptance rate 66%. This entry is a two-page poster summary. All four authors at Iowa State University. The poster prints no funding or conflict statement; ERIC and the IES award page tie it to IES award R305H190058 ($400,000, 2019–2024, Iowa State University with Des Moines Public Schools), a competitively reviewed public grant. The authors and university don't appear on IM's team, partners or research pages.
  3. Interactive Dynamics in Task-Based Curricular MaterialsAllison L. Gantt (University of Delaware). Proceedings of the 44th Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), pp. 132–136, 2022. Curriculum analysis.Qualitative content analysis of the teacher's guides for one unit each of IM Algebra 1 (Unit 2, Linear Equations, Inequalities, and Systems, 2019) and the Interactive Mathematics Program ("Cookies"); number of tasks not stated. The author reports that IM's guide emphasized building students' metacognitive awareness and precise mathematical language; the findings are described as preliminary.Proceedings front matter: "Each paper was reviewed by multiple referees in a double-blind process"; overall acceptance rate 66%. Single author at the University of Delaware. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The author and university don't appear on IM's team, partners or research pages.
  4. Dilating Perspectives: A Comparison of Teacher- and Student-Facing Texts on a Unit on SimilaritySoobin Jeon, Patricio G. Herbst (University of Michigan). Proceedings of the 45th Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), Vol. 1, pp. 291–299, 2023. Curriculum analysis.Linguistic analysis of the teacher-facing and student-facing text of IM Geometry's Unit on Similarity (16 lessons and a modeling prompt). The teacher-facing learning goals used more "mental (cognizing)" verbs, the student-facing goals more "material and verbal" ones.Proceedings preface gives acceptance rates (research reports 45%, brief research reports 70%, posters 90%) and the volume lists its reviewers; it does not describe the review process itself. Both authors at the University of Michigan. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The authors and university don't appear on IM's team, partners or research pages.

Each study is about this product, by researchers with no tie to its developer we could find.

Who funded it

NSF did not fund this product. It is listed because independent researchers studied it in peer-reviewed venues (see Research behind it). Funders named by the developer: The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, Carnegie Corporation of New York, Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, Heising-Simons Foundation, The William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, W.K. Kellogg Foundation. IM's About page thanks these philanthropic partners and says philanthropic support helps make its work possible; it does not say which funder paid for which course. IM 6–8 Math was originally developed by Open Up Resources and authored by IM.
