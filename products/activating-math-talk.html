What a teacher gets

Activating Math Talk is a Corwin book by Paola Sztajn, Daniel Heck and Kristen Malzahn, written from Project AIM, a professional development program on math discourse in early elementary classrooms. Corwin lists it for PreK-4; its classroom examples of tasks and techniques span grades K-5.

The book is framed around 11 student-centered discourse techniques, grouped by lesson phase. Three are for the launch (Story Problem Retelling, Task Think-Aloud, Math Bet Lines), four for the explore phase (among them Think-Pair-Rehearse-Share and Talk Triangle) and four for the discussion (among them Math Talk Chain and Probing and Pressing Math Questions). Each technique chapter opens with what the technique is and why you might use it, then covers getting started, supports, adapting it, and an Examine Practice section. Chapters end with Discuss With Colleagues questions and a Connect to Your Practice activity, and the book includes classroom vignettes and a focus on emergent multilingual learners. The NSF report says the book can be used alongside the Project AIM professional development or on its own.

How these awards connect to this product

The book's copyright page says "This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Grant Nos. EHR-1513155, EHR-1513104 and EHR-1020177." All three are confirmed in the NSF API: 1020177 is Project AIM: All Included in Mathematics (Paola Sztajn, North Carolina State University, 2010-2017), and 1513155 (Sztajn, NC State) and 1513104 (Daniel J Heck, Horizon Research) are the collaborative impact study of the Project AIM professional development (2015-2021). The NSF project outcomes report for 1513155 lists the book among the project's products: "the project leaders authored a commercially published book entitled Activating Math Talk." The acknowledgments footnote in the same sample prints the first number as "1021177"; the copyright page and the NSF record give 1020177, which we use.
