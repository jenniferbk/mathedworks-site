Before you use it

  • Paid: $89.95 on the WestEd store (retrieved Oct 25, 2026). The store page does not say how the video is delivered (format is listed only as "Multimedia"), so ask before buying.
  • Plan for 30 hours of facilitated sessions (10 three-hour sessions) with a group of teachers.
  • The kit includes video clips, PowerPoint presentations, handouts and GeoGebra applets for the sessions.
  • The 2017 edition (the page lists 70 pages) says it is aligned with the Common Core. The content is similarity, congruence and transformations, marketed for grades 6–12; the original NSF award described the work for middle-school teachers.
  • What is sold is a 10-session (30-hour) set; award 0732757 planned a larger program of a foundation module plus four extension modules, which the WestEd page we captured does not offer.

What a teacher gets

Learning and Teaching Geometry is a WestEd professional development package for leading teachers of grades 6–12 through the mathematics of similarity based on geometric transformations, and how to teach it. It runs as 10 three-hour sessions (30 hours in all). In each session participants work on mathematics, view and discuss video clips from unstaged classrooms, compare issues across cases, and connect them to their own teaching. The publisher lists a Facilitator Guide (print and PDF eBook), 27 video clips, and session agendas with detailed notes and mathematical commentary, PowerPoint presentations, embedded assessments, handouts and GeoGebra applets. The publisher says facilitators will find everything they need to lead the series of sessions. We did not see the materials themselves; this description comes from the publisher's page. NSF funded a cluster-randomized efficacy study of the materials (award 1503399); we did not review its results.

How these awards connect to this product

Award 0732757 (WestEd, PI Seago, co-PIs Driscoll and Callahan) has the product's name and describes video-case modules on similarity for middle-school teachers; Seago, Driscoll and Callahan are among the published authors. The award planned a foundation module of 6-8 sessions plus four extension modules; the WestEd page we captured describes only a 10-session set. Award 1503399 (University of Colorado Boulder, PI Jacobs) funded an efficacy study of the materials, not their development. The publisher page we captured does not mention NSF. Relation 'Funded this product': 0732757 carries the product's name, its PI and co-PIs are among the authors, and its abstract describes developing the LTG video-case modules.
