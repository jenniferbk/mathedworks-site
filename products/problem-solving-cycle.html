Before you use it

  • You will need to buy the print book; the NCTM store page showed $11.90 on Oct 25, 2026. The table of contents, preface and Chapter 1 are free to preview there.
  • This is a guide for PD leaders, not classroom materials. It describes the two models and gives examples of workshop activities for teachers and for PD leaders.
  • You will need classroom video: each teacher videotapes the lesson, and the facilitator picks clips from the unedited footage for Workshops 2 and 3.
  • NCTM describes the book as for K–12 mathematics teachers, but the models were developed and field-tested with middle school teachers.
  • The Facilitator's Guide the book points to (cset.stanford.edu/psc) returned "not found" on Oct 25, 2026. The book was published in 2015; its first chapter presents the PSC as a way to support initiatives such as the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics.

What a teacher gets

This 160-page book (Teachers College Press, copublished with NCTM, 2015) is written for school leaders and other PD providers. It presents two models: the Problem-Solving Cycle (PSC), a school-based professional learning community usually led by a teacher leader, and the Mathematics Leadership Preparation (MLP) model for preparing those leaders.

In each PSC cycle teachers work together on one mathematical problem and plan lessons in Workshop 1, then each teaches an adapted version of the problem to at least one class, where the lesson is videotaped. Workshops 2 and 3 use video clips the facilitator picks from that footage, plus student work, to analyze student thinking and instruction. The MLP model is an annual Summer Leadership Academy followed by Leader Support Meetings during the school year. Chapters give vignettes of both models in action (the Disc Problem and the Fuel Gauge Problem), summarize the developers' research, and present a case study of one district that used both models.

How these awards connect to this product

The book's preface says both models were developed and field-tested in two NSF-supported projects but gives no award numbers: STAAR ("Supporting the Transition from Arithmetic to Algebraic Reasoning"), where the PSC took shape from 2003, and iPSC, begun in 2007. The iPSC title in the preface matches award 0732212 (Jacobs, Koellner). We could not identify the STAAR award: the closest NSF record we found, Borko's 0115609 ("Understanding and Cultivating the Transition from Arithmetic to Algebraic Reasoning"), has a different title and its abstract does not describe the PSC, so it is not listed. A later related project, award 1417261 (Borko, Carlson), is credited on the archived Stanford CSET page for PSC and teacher-leader work with San Francisco Unified; it did not fund the book and is not listed.
