Before you use it

  • Full access is paid and no price is posted; contact The Math Door for a demo and pricing.
  • A free Guest Log In opens six learning trajectories, two demo tests and a sample student report for ratio. NC State announced Math-Mapper free to the public during the COVID-19 school closures in April 2020, but according to the site only six trajectories and two demo tests are now public and full access must be purchased, so that offer appears to have ended.
  • It runs in a browser; the developers recommend the latest Chrome (preferred), Safari or Firefox.
  • The app's version line reads v5.1.1 (09/29/2020), and how it is maintained now is not stated.
  • Test length and standards alignment are not stated on the pages we saved; the NSF record for 1621254 ties the map's content ideas to the Common Core.

What a teacher gets

Math-Mapper 6-8 is a web-based formative assessment system from The Math Door, a start-up affiliated with North Carolina State University, developed under Jere Confrey. It is built around a learning map of the big ideas of middle school math, and a 2020 NC State news item says its trajectories cover all the big ideas of the middle grades. The developers' site counts nine big ideas; the NSF outcomes report for award 1621254 counts eleven, across the middle grades and Algebra 1. Under each sit learning trajectories, which the developers describe as research-based landmarks and obstacles along students' paths to understanding.

Teachers give diagnostic tests that are scored in real time and get individual and class reports; students see their own results. A "Resourcer" links to web resources curated by teachers, and a "Sequencer" lines up the assessments with a school's scope and sequence. The developers pitch the trajectories for teacher study groups as well as for testing.

How these awards connect to this product

The Math-Mapper site acknowledges NSF grants DRL1118858 and DRL1621254, plus Gates Foundation and NewSchools Venture Fund support. In the NSF API, 1118858 (Confrey, NC State, 2011-2017) built and validated learning trajectories and assessment items for fraction, ratio, and decimal and percent in grades 3-7, and 1621254 (Confrey, 2016-2020) set out to build a next-generation diagnostic assessment and reporting system on a learning-trajectory map for grades 6-8.
