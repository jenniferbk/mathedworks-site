What a teacher gets

Decluttering Mathematics is a book for teachers and instructional coaches about what mathematics matters most in grades 6–12. The publisher's description says secondary instruction is often cluttered with disconnected procedures, jargon and drill, and offers an alternative built on five fundamental understandings, which include measurement, comparisons, proportional relationships, equivalence and graphical representations. The book describes how these understandings unleash student thinking and gives practical examples. A feature called The Math Attic recommends the clutter that can be de-prioritized in light of the five understandings.

The publisher says the book draws on mathematics education research and on an NSF-funded project involving over 300 educators, and writes for veteran and preservice teachers alike. Teachers College Press publishes it in its Math Teaching in Practice Series, co-published with the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics.

Who made it

Arizona Mathematics Partnership (AMP). Developed by Ted Coe, Scott Adamson, April Strom and James Tanton.

How these awards connect to this product

The book's publisher says it draws on "an NSF-funded project involving over 300 educators" but names no award, and we have not seen the book's acknowledgments. We tie it to 1103080 by inference from the NSF record. April Strom, one of the four authors, was PI of 1103080, the Arizona Mathematics Partnership (AMP, 2012-2018), a Math and Science Partnership award for middle school mathematics. Its outcomes report says over 300 in-service middle school mathematics teachers, school and district administrators and pre-service teachers in the Phoenix area took part, consistent with the publisher's "over 300 educators" (both are round figures). The award's reported publications include a 2016 article by Scott Adamson, another author, on using the idea of steepness to develop mathematical reasoning; Adamson is not a listed PI or co-PI on 1103080, only the author of that reported article. The record does not mention the book, Ted Coe or James Tanton.
