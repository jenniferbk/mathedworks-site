Interactive Mathematics Program (IMP), Years 1-4

A four-year, problem-based integrated high school curriculum built around unit problems, still sold in print and e-book by It's About Time (Activate Learning).

  • Grades 9–12
  • Paid

Before you use it

  • It is paid and no prices are posted: the publisher sells print student and teacher materials, an e-book and professional learning, as a package or individually, and directs buyers to "Connect with an Expert".
  • Graphing calculators are an integral part of the curriculum.
  • Plan on substantial teaching time; the one teacher's guide activity we read is planned for 70 minutes, and total course pacing is not stated in the sources we saved.
  • Editions differ. The 2010-era sampler we read puts Patterns and The Game of Pig in Year 1 and a unit called The Diver Returns in Year 4; the current product page lists a different sequence, with Cookies and All About Alice in Year 1 and The Game of Pig in Year 2. The sampler also describes a Traditional Pathway reordering (Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2) and individual unit books; check which version you are buying.
  • The sampler cites the Common Core and PARCC; it dates from about 2010.

Try one lesson

Profitable Pictures (Cookies unit, 2nd edition) web.archive.org

  • High school
  • Using the Feasible Region
  • About 55 minutes
  • Free, no login

Students help Hassan decide how many pastels and watercolors to paint: they find combinations within his materials and picture limits that earn exactly $1,000, $500 and $600, plot them on the feasible region, then find the maximum profit and write an explanation convincing his mother it is the maximum.

You need: Graph paper and the Profitable Pictures blackline master.

This 2010 second-edition activity is an archived copy from the IMP developers' site, and it builds on the previous activity, Picturing Pictures, in which students find the feasible region.

What a teacher gets

The Interactive Mathematics Program is an integrated, college-preparatory high school curriculum in which each unit is organized around a central problem set in a real-world or literary context. The current publisher page lists five units per year: Year 1 includes The Overland Trail (linear relationships), The Pit and the Pendulum (standard deviation) and Cookies (maximizing a bakery's profits with equations, inequalities and their graphs); later years cover quadratics (Fireworks), geometry by construction and proof, probability, circles and coordinate geometry, trigonometry (High Dive), functions, statistical inference, and derivatives and integrals (Small World, Isn't It?; How Much? How Fast?).

The publisher's sampler shows how a unit runs. Cookies opens with the unit problem, students work through activity clusters in groups with whole-class discussion, return to the unit problem, and finish by compiling a portfolio that includes a problem of the week. Teacher's guide notes for each activity give its intent, the mathematics, approximate time (70 minutes for the first Cookies activity), classroom organization and discussion questions; units come with in-class and take-home assessments. Graphing calculators are built into the curriculum.

How these awards connect to this product

The publisher and its sampler say IMP was designed and field-tested with NSF support but give no award numbers. The three awards listed are the ones whose NSF records describe IMP itself - 9050208 (new problem-based high school curriculum built around units, 1990), 9255262 (Phase II, adding a fourth year and revising the first three) and 0137805 (IMP for the 21st Century, a revision adding technology and content changes). No source says which award funded the edition sold today. The last award ended in 2006; the sampler we read is a 2010 second-edition text, and no source says NSF funded the editions sold now, so the relation is recorded as an earlier edition.
