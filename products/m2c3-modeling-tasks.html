Before you use it

  • Free to download with no login. The site states no license; the pages we saved carry a University of Arizona copyright notice, and the task files are marked "M2C3 Project (2018)".
  • You'll need a projector for the lesson slides; some slides link to YouTube videos, so you'll need internet access and a school that allows YouTube.
  • Some tasks call for hands-on materials. Designing a Garden, for example, lists color tiles, chart paper and markers, with dirt (or sand or small beads) and measuring tools optional.
  • Plan time per task: the Abuelo's Birthday overview calls for a 60-90 minute full modeling task. Some tasks come in two versions; Abuelo's Birthday has an easier Version A and a Version B with an income range.
  • The materials date from 2018 and list standards with CCSS-style codes. The project developed its professional development model with teacher cohorts in Arizona and Washington state.

What a teacher gets

The M2C3 project posts mathematical modeling tasks for grades 3-5 that draw on students' home, school and community lives. Tasks are grouped by context: gardening, home and family activities (such as sharing the cost of a grandparent's birthday gift or comparing showers and baths for water use), community activities and businesses (cookie sales, guacamole pricing, pupusa making), local school activities, selecting sports teams, and sharing snacks.

A typical task has a lesson overview, slides, and the student task in PDF and editable Word; many include student work. The two lesson overviews we read list grade 3, 4 and 5 standards, using CCSS-style codes and the Standards for Mathematical Practice, prior knowledge, cultural and family connections, opening routines (notice and wonder), the task parts, anticipated student strategies and assumptions, and materials. Designing a Garden, for example, has four parts: planning a 4-by-8-foot plot, buying compost, fencing, and writing a funding application. A Tools and Resources page adds planning sheets for designing modeling tasks from community walks or from existing curriculum, routines for noticing and assumption building, and a lesson planning template.

How these awards connect to this product

The M2C3 home page shows an NSF logo labeled "NSF Project: EHR #1561274" (the image's alt text gives the same words); no page text mentions NSF. Award 1561274 is Mary Q. Foote's award at CUNY Queens College, one of four 2016 collaborative NSF awards titled Mathematical Modeling with Cultural and Community Contexts, one to each of the four principal investigators the site names (Aguirre, Turner, Foote and Roth McDuffie), at the institutions the site lists. Their records describe research on modeling in grades 3-5 and say teachers will adapt modeling tasks and design lessons around them. Because the site credits this NSF project and the task files (dated 2018) fall within the award period, we record NSF as funding the tasks; no source says which of the four awards paid for which files. The follow-on 2020 awards (2008997, 2010178, 2010202, Advancing Equity and Strengthening Teaching with Elementary Mathematical Modeling) build on this work, but their records describe a professional development program and do not mention these tasks, so they are not listed.
