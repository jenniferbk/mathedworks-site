Before you use it

  • Paid. Lab-Aids does not post prices on the grade pages we captured; they offer "Request a Quote", and a separate Lab-Aids form takes requests for a free sample or preview. The pages do not say whether single classrooms can order or only districts. We saw no sample content; that form is the route to see the materials.
  • Online portal licenses run six years per teacher or student. The digital Student Edition comes through the online student portal, which includes Desmos, MathType and read-aloud tools; print editions are also sold.
  • Time: Lab-Aids lists 135 (grade 6), 136.5 (grade 7) and 138 (grade 8) class periods; the pages do not say how long a period is. Prep: Lab-Aids sells optional classroom kits with reusable card sorts and game boards to reduce preparation; the core program does not require them.
  • Edition differences: Earlier CMP editions were published by Pearson. Lab-Aids introduced it at NCTM in September 2024, so materials, pacing and portal features may differ from older CMP materials a school already owns.
  • Grade 8 is labeled "8th Grade & Algebra" and integrates Algebra I/High School Course I standards; check fit with your district's course sequence. Lab-Aids lists support for multilingual learners; we found no Spanish edition.

What a teacher gets

Connected Mathematics 4 is the fourth edition of the Connected Mathematics Project curriculum, developed at Michigan State University and introduced by its new publisher, Lab-Aids, in 2024; earlier editions were published by Pearson. It is a full middle-school program built around sequenced contextual problems. Lessons follow a Launch-Explore-Summarize model, and class discussion focuses on the strategies students used, the mathematics in the problem and how it connects to prior work.

Lab-Aids lists 7 units (135 periods) for grade 6, 8 units (136.5 periods) for grade 7, and 8 units (138 periods) for grade 8, which integrates Algebra I/High School Course I standards. Teachers get a print set of Teacher Editions with A Guide to Connected Mathematics 4 and/or a six-year online teacher portal for assigning ACE problem sets, reflection questions and assessments. Students get print Student Editions or a six-year online portal; Lab-Aids also sells classroom material kits (card sorts, game boards) and student notebooks. We did not see the materials; this card is drawn from publisher and university descriptions.

How these awards connect to this product

MSU and Lab-Aids say Connected Mathematics was developed at MSU with NSF support but do not name awards. 9150217 ("Connected Mathematics Project", 1991-1997; PI William M. Fitzgerald, with Lappan, Phillips, Fey and Friel as co-PIs) funded development of the original three-year middle-school curriculum, and 9986372 ("Connected Mathematics Phase II", 2000-2007; PI Lappan) funded its revision. 2007842 (2020-2025, PI Phillips) funded research and development of a digital collaborative platform that, per its outcomes report, embeds the CMP4 curriculum and Teacher Guide and was released as open-source code; no source we found says this platform is the Lab-Aids portal sold with CMP4 or that the award funded writing CMP4 itself. 2200763 (2022-2027, PI Phillips) continues that platform work using Connected Mathematics Project materials and is not listed. Relation set to 'Funded an earlier edition', the weaker reading, because no NSF record shows funding for developing CMP4.
