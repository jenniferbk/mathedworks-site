Math Pathways & Pitfalls lessons

Four Second Edition books of K–8 discussion lessons in which students compare a correct answer with a common mistake ("pitfall") and explain why it doesn't make sense.

Before you use it

  • Each of the four Second Edition books (K–1, 2–3, 4–6, 6–8; copyright 2024) is listed at $79.95; order online or by purchase order through the WestEd Publications Center. Book contents are delivered partly through an online resource library.
  • Free to download from the site are a Common Core crosswalk and one sample lesson per grade, K–8. The sample lesson we read is marked © 2010, so it may differ from the 2024 edition.
  • Plan on some prep: for the sample lesson, staple a packet of student pages 1–4 for each student, copy and cut pages 5 and 6, and post the Discussion Builders poster; number lines and fraction pieces are suggested, and a projector is optional. Plan two consecutive 45-minute days for each core lesson, then a 20-minute multiple-choice mini-lesson and a 25-minute writing mini-lesson (1–2 days later per the site; the grade 4 sample says 2–3 days). Kindergarten and grade 1 lessons are shorter.
  • The program does not cover every standard; the developers say it addresses the most important ones in each grade and leave it to you to choose and order the lessons. Optional professional development (1–3 day institutes, workshops, trainer-of-trainer institutes) is offered, but the developers say the books can be used right away.
  • WestEd sells Spanish versions of the Discussion Builders posters; we found no Spanish lessons for sale, although the original 2000 NSF award planned the materials in English and Spanish.

Try one lesson

Grade 6: Unit 6, Lesson 5: Changing Ratios to Percents mpp.wested.org

  • Grade 6
  • Changing Ratios to Percents
  • Two consecutive 45-minute days, then a 20-minute and a 25-minute mini-lesson 2–3 days later
  • Free, no login

Students write 24 out of 60 kids as a percent, then discuss two imaginary students' work: Abby simplifies to 2/5 and gets 40%, while Walt divides 24 into 60 and gets 25%, a pitfall. On day 2 they practice in Our Turn and My Turn pages.

You need: A stapled packet of Student Pages 1–4 per student, Student Pages 5 and 6 copied and cut in half, and the Discussion Builders poster; calculators are suggested and a projector is optional.

The sample is marked © 2010 WestEd, so it may differ from the Second Edition (2024) now sold.

What a teacher gets

Math Pathways & Pitfalls is a WestEd intervention and prevention program for grades K–8, sold as four books (grades K–1, 2–3, 4–6 and 6–8). Each book has 20-plus lessons with a teaching manual, Discussion Builders classroom posters with sentence starters (also in Spanish) and professional development materials; buyers get the student pages and teaching guides through an online resource library. The books cover whole number, place value and operations in the early grades, fractions and decimals in grades 4–6, and percents, ratios and proportions in grades 6–8, all with algebra readiness.

The free grade 4 sample lesson (Adding Fractions) runs two days. On day 1 the class discusses a starter problem and two pieces of "student thinking", one correct and one a pitfall; on day 2 students review and practice in Our Turn and My Turn pages. Two to three days later come a multiple-choice mini-lesson and a writing-task mini-lesson. The program can be used with the whole class alongside the core curriculum, in small groups, after school or in tutoring.

How these awards connect to this product

The Math Pathways & Pitfalls site credits NSF grant ESI-9911374, along with the Stuart Foundation and an IES grant, for the program's materials and research. The NSF record for 9911374 (Barnett-Clarke, WestEd) describes developing K-8 lessons in which students discuss why incorrect work is wrong. Two later WestEd awards name the program in their titles but are not credited on the site; 0918834 built online professional development modules on the lessons, and 1314416 set out to develop an MPP book on algebra readiness for the middle grades. No source we saved says whether that book is the current grades 6-8 volume. 9550063 (Barnett-Clarke's case-discussion project for teachers) does not name the program and is not listed. The NSF record for 9911374 carries the bare title "Kids". The site does not say which edition 9911374 funded. All three awards ended before the edition now sold (9911374 in 2007, 0918834 in 2016, 1314416 in 2019; the Second Edition is copyright 2024), so we record the relation as funding an earlier edition. The NSF-funded lessons may carry into the Second Edition, but no source we saved says so.
