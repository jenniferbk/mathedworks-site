Before you use it

  • Heinemann sells these books only to educational institutions: the pages we saved mark them as restricted products that can be bought only on behalf of an educational institution, through an Institutional account, so you'll likely need to order through your school or district. You will need to buy the books (paperback). On Oct 25, 2026 Heinemann listed the fractions volume at $37.33 ($28.00 web/school price), the six-volume set at $196.67 ($147.50), Geometric Shapes at $40.67 and the 432-page Geometric Measurement at $66.67. The fractions, shapes and measurement books are printed on demand, nonreturnable, and take about a week to print.
  • You can preview before buying: the fractions volume page has a free sample (front matter and Chapter 1), extra assessment tasks, and 53 student sheets you may photocopy for classroom or workshop use.
  • Grade fit differs by book: Heinemann lists the fractions volume and the set as K–6 and the two geometry volumes as grades 1–8.
  • Budget time for interviews. The author calls one-on-one interviews the most accurate way to place students; the alternative is a whole-class task where students write their methods and then discuss them.
  • The books date from 2012.

What a teacher gets

Cognition-Based Assessment & Teaching is a six-book series by Michael Battista, published by Heinemann in 2012: Addition and Subtraction, Multiplication and Division, Place Value, Fractions, Geometric Shapes, and Geometric Measurement. Heinemann's blurb for the fractions volume and the set says the series is designed to work with any curriculum.

We examined the fractions volume's free sample and companion files. Chapter 2 describes levels of sophistication in students' reasoning about fractions, with many examples of student work. Chapter 3 offers instructional suggestions for moving students from one level to the next; the author says these are not complete treatments of topics. The appendix holds the CBA assessment tasks, each followed by what each level of reasoning might look like on that task, and Heinemann's site adds extra tasks and photocopiable student sheets. The author recommends giving tasks in individual interviews as the most accurate method, and also describes using a task as a whole-class activity followed by discussion.

How these awards connect to this product

The fractions volume's acknowledgments say research and development for CBA was supported in part by NSF grants 0099047, 0352898, 554470 and 838137. The last two are printed without their leading zero; the NSF records 0554470 and 0838137 are Battista's awards on elementary teachers' learning and use of research-based materials, so we read them as those awards. 0099047 and 0352898 are Battista's awards to develop a cognition-based assessment system. Only the fractions volume's front matter was checked; the acknowledgments in the other five volumes were not seen. The acknowledgment says NSF supported CBA research and development "in part" and does not say which parts, so we use the partial relation.
