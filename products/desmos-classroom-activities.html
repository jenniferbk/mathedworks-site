Before you use it

  • The activities and Activity Builder are free. We couldn't confirm from a saved page whether you need an account to run an activity with a class.

What a teacher gets

Amplify Classroom, formerly Desmos Classroom, is a free online platform with hundreds of ready-made lessons and activities; Amplify has since added literacy and science, and this card covers the math. Its math collections run from kindergarten through grade 12, and many of the math lessons come from Amplify's paid Amplify Desmos Math curriculum. The older Desmos activities, such as Marbleslides and Polygraph, are still free, and teachers can customize them or build their own with Activity Builder. Amplify describes the platform as Desmos Studio's math tools coupled with instruction and collaboration tools.

Desmos built the platform; in 2022 Amplify acquired it with the team behind it, while the calculators stayed with Desmos Studio (see that card). Three peer-reviewed studies by university researchers, in PME-NA proceedings and two refereed journals, look at Desmos activities in a middle school program, a teacher-preparation course and an analysis of the teacher platform (listed below).

Who made it

Amplify Classroom, the free activity platform Amplify acquired from Desmos in 2022 (teacher.desmos.com). Developed by Desmos curriculum and classroom team, now at Amplify (no individual lead named in saved sources).

Research behind it

  1. Integrated STEM Education Through Game-Based LearningMicah Stohlmann (University of Nevada, Las Vegas). Proceedings of the 42nd Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education (PME-NA), pp. 2238–2242, 2020. Other.Teaching experiment with 19 middle school students (ages 11–15) in a five-week Saturday STEM program, playing the Desmos activity Polygraph lines. Through the game and later discussion, students used vocabulary including slope, horizontal and vertical lines, origin and quadrants.Proceedings front matter: submissions were reviewed "in a double-blind process by three (sometimes four) colleagues." Single author at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas. The paper prints no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. The author doesn't appear in the saved Desmos Fellows lists (cohorts 2–4).
  2. Affordances of Virtual Learning Environments to Support Mathematics TeachingJana Trgalová (Haute École Pédagogique, Lausanne), Michal Tabach (Tel Aviv University). Digital Experiences in Mathematics Education, 9, 444–475, 2023. Other.Analysis of four teaching platforms (STEP, Desmos, WIMS, Labomep) for how they support teachers in planning and running technology-based lessons. The platforms support teachers during lessons, and teachers need to learn to base decisions on the data shown in the platforms' dashboards.Journal's review-process page: single anonymous peer review by at least two reviewers after an editorial suitability check. Authors at a Swiss teacher-education university and Tel Aviv University. Funding statement covers open-access fees only (HEP Vaud); "The authors declare no competing interests." Neither author appears in the saved Desmos Fellows lists (cohorts 2–4).
  3. Prospective Teachers' Cognitive Engagement during Virtual Teaching Using GeoGebra and DesmosSolomon A. Tesfamicael (Norwegian University of Science and Technology). Pythagoras: Journal of the Association for Mathematics Education of South Africa, 43(1), a691, 2022. Teacher perceptions or use.Case study of a second-year teacher-preparation course in statistics and probability taught online from January to May 2022, drawn from two teacher educators' reflections and screenshots of student work. Number of prospective teachers not stated; the author reports that GeoGebra's Class function and the Desmos Teacher feature let instructors observe students' cognitive engagement in real time.Journal information page: "The journal has a double-blinded peer review process," with two expert independent reviewers. Single author at NTNU. "This research received no specific grant from any funding agency"; the author declares no financial or personal relationships that may have influenced the article. The author doesn't appear in the saved Desmos Fellows lists (cohorts 2–4).

Each study is about this product, by researchers with no tie to its developer we could find.

Who funded it

NSF did not fund this product. It is listed because independent researchers studied it in peer-reviewed venues (see Research behind it). No saved source names a funder. Amplify, a publisher of curriculum and assessment, runs the free platform and sells Amplify Desmos Math, a curriculum built on it.
