ESTEEM e-modules

Free online modules, over 40 hours in all, that prepare future middle and high school math teachers to teach statistics with real data and CODAP, ready to import into a course site.

  • Grades 6–12
  • Free with registration
  • Internet, Specific software

Get the materialsfi.ncsu.edu

Before you use it

  • These are teacher-education materials, not a K–12 curriculum: the intended users are instructors of future or practicing grades 6–12 math teachers.
  • Getting the files takes an account. The materials page offers two routes: a free registration at the Friday Institute's PLACE site, or Canvas Commons. The PLACE link now redirects to a general list of Friday Institute online courses (checked Oct 25, 2026), and downloading from Canvas Commons requires signing in.
  • The Canvas Commons copy is a Canvas course export released under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike license. The Friday Institute page says the materials can be downloaded for direct import into a learning management system.
  • The modules use CODAP, a free browser-based data tool, so participants need a device with a web browser and internet access.
  • The ESTEEM I materials were posted to Canvas Commons in 2023. Updated ESTEEM II module sets are now there too, under the same license: Foundations in Data Science and Statistics Teaching (posted March 2025) and Teaching Statistical Association (posted July 2025); the Inference set is listed as coming soon. The Friday Institute materials page still says the update is coming in 2024.

Try one lesson

Are Granola Bars Healthy? codap.concord.org

  • Grades 6–12
  • Relationships between attributes of granola bars
  • Time not stated
  • Free, no login

Students explore USDA data on 33 granola bars in CODAP, decide which attributes matter for a 'healthy' bar, then graph Nuts against Texture with counts and percents to judge whether the two are related, and go on to other relationships, such as whether organic bars have less corn syrup.

The CODAP file opens with a two-page guide credited as a product of ESTEEM; the guide states no grade or time, so the grade band is the one ESTEEM gives for its modules.

What a teacher gets

ESTEEM is a set of online modules for teacher educators to use with undergraduate or graduate students preparing to teach middle or secondary mathematics; the developers say instructors can adapt them for face-to-face or hybrid courses or for other teacher audiences. The first round of the project produced over 40 hours of material, shared as a course you can import into a learning management system. Module 1 (Foundations in Statistics Teaching, parts 1.1 and 1.2) comes first; then Teaching Inferential Reasoning (A.1, A.2) and Teaching Statistical Association (B.1, B.2) can be used in either order. Two assignments, task design and screencast, let teachers demonstrate what they learned.

Each part follows the same sections: Read & Watch, Learn from Practice, Engage with Data and Synthesize and Apply. Teachers investigate real data sets (roller coasters, dice fairness, Paul the Octopus, vehicle emissions, granola bars) and use CODAP. They also examine student work and take quizzes; item titles include an expert teacher interview and a statistical investigation cycle in a classroom. Part 1.1 lists a time expectation of 7–8 hours.

How these awards connect to this product

The NSF record for 1625713 (Lee, NC State, 2016) is the ESTEEM project and describes creating online resources and modules for preparing secondary mathematics teachers to teach statistics; an ESTEEM lesson plan credits DUE 1625713. The materials on this card are the ESTEEM I materials from that award. A follow-on award, 2141727 (Lee, NC State, 2022), matches the ESTEEM II team and start date on the project page, but its NSF title ("Enhancing Data Science and Statistics Teacher Education--Transforming and Building Community") and abstract do not name ESTEEM. The updated ESTEEM II module sets (Foundations, 2025; Association, 2025) are on Canvas Commons, but no source we saved names the award that paid for them, so 2141727 is not listed.
