Before you use it

  • Free; the simulations need no sign-in. They run in a web browser and can also be downloaded, according to PhET's About page; PhET says its HTML5 simulations run on iPads, Chromebooks, PCs, Macs and Linux.
  • On Equality Explorer, the one simulation page we checked, the About tab lists topics, sample learning goals and Common Core standards from 6.EE.B.5 to HSA-REI.B.3, and the Teaching Resources tab has alignment documents. Its Teacher Tips panel asks you to sign in to a PhET account; a Teacher Tips PDF link is also listed, which we did not open. The Activities tab lists 11 activities, two marked From PhET, such as a 90-minute activity sheet; we did not review their quality.
  • PhET groups simulations by school level (elementary, middle, high school, university); check the listed levels for each one, since many span two or more.
  • Some simulations in the Math Applications group are mainly physics (for example, Ohm's Law and Pendulum Lab).
  • PhET's About page says some legacy simulations are in Java or Flash; the 51 counted here are all HTML5.

What a teacher gets

PhET, at the University of Colorado Boulder, makes free interactive simulations for science and mathematics. On Oct 25, 2026, PhET listed 51 HTML5 simulations in its Math & Statistics category, most tagged Math Concepts and some tagged Math Applications, which include physics simulations such as Pendulum Lab. Titles run from Make a Ten and Number Play (elementary) through Fractions: Intro, Area Model Multiplication, Proportion Playground and Unit Rates, Equality Explorer and Expression Exchange, Graphing Lines and Graphing Quadratics, Center and Variability, up to Calculus Grapher and Least-Squares Regression.

Each simulation is a self-contained interactive model: students drag objects, move sliders and choose options, and see immediate feedback. PhET says it tests each simulation through student interviews and classroom observation, and its design principles include multiple representations and implicit guidance through limited controls. The simulations are open source.

How these awards connect to this product

The NSF record for 1503510 (Perkins, University of Colorado Boulder, 2015) is a DRK-12 project using PhET simulations to teach algebraic thinking in grades 6–9, and says it will produce 15 research-based, student-tested simulations with instructional support and teacher PD resources. No source we saved says which of the current math simulations came from that award. PhET's other NSF awards on the shortlist (0442841, 0817582, 1020362, 1503439, 1621363) are not listed, because their records describe physics, chemistry or science simulations, not the math suite. Among the 51 math-category simulations, the PhET metadata credits an NSF grant (STROBE, DMR-1548924) only on Fourier (Making Waves), which PhET also files under physics.
