Math Snacks games and animations

Free online animations and games for grades 4–8 on ratio, number, place value and early algebra, each with a printable teacher guide, and learner guides for the animations.

  • Grades 3–8
  • Free, no login
  • Projector, Device per student, Internet
  • Item bank, Game

Get the materialsmathsnacks.com

Before you use it

  • Free, with no cost or login mentioned; the NSF record says all Math Snacks are freely accessible online.
  • Technology: animations need a computer, a projector and Internet; games need a computer for each student. The Curse Reverse guide recommends Firefox. Ratio Rumble loaded on a desktop browser in October 2026; the games have not been tested on iPads or Chromebooks.
  • Time: two class periods (Bad Date), two 40-minute game sessions plus a 30-minute bonus activity (Ratio Rumble), or four sessions of about 20 to 45 minutes (Curse Reverse).
  • Prep: watch the teaching-with video, play the game yourself and copy the learner guides; for Curse Reverse, print keys and posters and gather dice, snap cubes and tape.
  • Grade fit and age: the guides cite Common Core standards for grades 4–7. The site's copyright runs 2009–2023 and the later NSF award ended in 2021. Older guides point to mathsnacks.org, which still loads, and to iTunes U for iPad downloads.

What a teacher gets

Math Snacks is a set of short animations (such as Bad Date and Atlantean Dodgeball on ratios, Number Rights on the number line, Scale Ella on scale factor) and games (such as Ratio Rumble, Pearl Diver, Monster School Bus, and the early-algebra games Agrinautica, Curse Reverse and Creature Caverns). The developers describe it as activities to use with the curriculum you already teach in grades 4–8, not a curriculum.

Each snack has a printable teacher guide with discussion questions, vocabulary and companion activities and a short "teaching with" video; each animation also has a learner guide and comic-book transcript, and the guides we read list Common Core standards. In the Ratio Rumble guide, students play for two 40-minute sessions with a discussion break, before any ratio instruction, and then organize equivalent ratios into a table. The Bad Date guide has the class watch the animation twice, pausing to discuss each ratio. Animations and student materials are also in Spanish.

How these awards connect to this product

The Math Snacks site says its materials were developed with support from NSF awards 0918794 and 1503507. 0918794 (Wiburg) describes developing 15–20 short animations and games for problematic middle-grades topics; 1503507 (Math Snacks Early Algebra) describes developing games for early algebra in grades 4–6. The site also credits a U.S. Department of Education cooperative agreement for prototyping some materials.
