Before you use it

  • Paid book: Heinemann lists it at $49.33 ($37.00 web/school price) for print with online video, with eBook and print-plus-eBook versions (checked Oct 25, 2026). Heinemann's page calls it a restricted product that can only be bought on behalf of an educational institution, through an institutional account, so you may need to order through your school.
  • You'll need internet access for the videos, which are linked by short web addresses printed beside the text. The two we tried from the sample chapter loaded on Oct 25, 2026.
  • There are no lesson plans or student pages. You write your own story problems from the book's problem-type framework.
  • Best read with colleagues: chapters include questions for reflection, with answers to selected ones in Appendix B. Heinemann also sells a separate Guide for Workshop Leaders.

What a teacher gets

Children's Mathematics is the main teacher book on Cognitively Guided Instruction (CGI). The second edition (Heinemann lists 2014; the book's copyright page says 2015; first edition 1999) has 13 chapters. Five cover how children solve addition, subtraction, multiplication and division problems, base-ten concepts and multidigit strategies; two cover classroom practice: posing problems and eliciting thinking, and engaging students with each other's ideas. Appendices cover the research base and answers to selected questions for reflection. Heinemann lists it for kindergarten through grade 5.

It is not a curriculum. The authors say they do not give specific directions for organizing a classroom or running instruction. What a teacher gets is a framework of problem types and children's strategies to guide which problems to pose and what to listen for. We read the sample, Chapter 9: it walks through posing and unpacking a story problem and follow-up questioning, using classroom episodes, with ten video clips linked in the margins. Heinemann lists over 90 online video episodes; the 1999 first edition came with CD-ROMs instead.

How these awards connect to this product

The only source tie is ERIC's record for the 1999 first edition: its HTML record names the National Science Foundation as sponsor, and the scanned ERIC document resume (not the HTML record, which lists no numbers) gives contracts MDR-8955346 and MDR-8550236. 8550236 is the 1985-1990 Fennema/Carpenter research award on applying cognitive science to mathematics instruction, which preceded the book. 8955346 is the Rational Number Project award (Post, University of Minnesota) in the NSF API, so it cannot be used. Other CGI awards (8954679, 9450076, 9911679) are not tied to the book by any source. No source says NSF funded the 2015 second edition.
