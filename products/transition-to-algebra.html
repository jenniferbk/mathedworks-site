Transition to Algebra

A 12-unit algebra support course of puzzles, mental math and explorations for students struggling with first-year algebra, sold by Heinemann as student worktexts and teaching guides.

  • Grades 6–12
  • Paid
  • No devices needed
  • Full curriculum, Teacher guide

Before you use it

  • Paid: Heinemann's Class Pack for 10 (worktexts for 10 students plus teacher materials) listed at $1,492.07, or $1,119.05 at the web/school price (checked Oct 25, 2026). Packs of 20 and 30 and a worktext replacement pack are sold separately. Heinemann sells the pack only on behalf of an educational institution, through an institutional account.
  • We examined one free sample unit (Unit 5 worktext and teaching guide) and Heinemann's descriptions; the other 11 units were not checked.
  • Plan for a separate class period. The developers built the course to run alongside a regular Algebra 1 class, not to replace it; Heinemann also suggests summer school use.
  • Prep in the Unit 5 sample: student groups cut out card sets from the worktext, and some displays come from a Resources PDF. Reproducible pages may be photocopied for classroom use only.
  • Materials are copyright 2014. Heinemann publishes a correlation to the Common Core Standards for Mathematical Practice.

Try one lesson

Lesson 1: Staying Balanced heinemann.com

  • Grades 6–10
  • The logic of solving mobile puzzles and algebraic equations
  • About 45 minutes: Mental Mathematics (5 min), launch (10 min), problem solving and discussion (30 min)
  • Free, no login

In groups, students sort Mobile Sorting Cards into YES (we know this mobile balances) and NO piles, then work through problems that translate balanced mobiles into equations and discuss why some changes keep a mobile, or an equation, balanced and others don't.

You need: Scissors and one set of Mobile Sorting Cards for each group of two or three students (Student Worktext page 55, also in the Resources PDF).

The free sample is from the Unit 5 Student Worktext and Teaching Guide; only pages marked with the Transition to Algebra copyright line may be photocopied, for classroom use only.

What a teacher gets

Transition to Algebra is a support course from Education Development Center (EDC) for students who are underprepared for Algebra 1. The developers designed it as a full-year course taken alongside first-year algebra. Instead of reteaching algebra the same way, it uses logic puzzles, problems and explorations to move students from arithmetic procedures toward algebraic reasoning. There are 12 units, from Language of Algebra to Algebraic Habits of Mind, with 80 lessons and 20 explorations across the series.

Each unit has a student worktext, a teaching guide and an answer key. In the Unit 5 sample we examined, the teaching guide lists eight lessons, two explorations, a related activity, student reflections, a Snapshot Check-in and a unit assessment. Lesson 1 there opens with five minutes of Mental Mathematics, then a 10-minute card-sorting launch and 30 minutes of problem solving and discussion. Worktext lessons are tiered into Important Stuff, Stuff to Make You Think, Tough Stuff and Additional Practice. Heinemann lists the series for grades 6–10.

How these awards connect to this product

The Unit 5 Teaching Guide states the material is based on work supported under NSF Grant No. ESI-0917958, the EDC development award for Transition to Algebra. Award 1621011 is a later study of how schools implement Transition to Algebra; its record describes research on the course, not development of the materials.
