OGAP (Ongoing Assessment Project) formative assessment system and professional learning

Research-based K–8 formative assessment system: learning progressions, item banks and district professional learning for reading student work, sold through BW Walch.

Before you use it

  • District purchase through BW Walch; prices are not posted. Evidence for ESSA estimates a district budget of about $430,000 to replicate the multi-cohort, multi-strand model used in the Philadelphia study across all relevant teachers. Walch sells to districts through a sales conversation; we found no individual-teacher purchase option. Routledge books by OGAP authors are sold separately.
  • Built for cohorts: about 24 educators per strand, four onsite days of professional learning plus two onsite coaching days. Districts typically provide release time.
  • No devices or software required; materials are handed out during the professional learning sessions. OGAP is not a curriculum. It is designed to work with whatever curriculum a school already uses.
  • The randomized evidence covers grades 3–5 (multiplicative and fractional strands) only; the K–2 and 6–8 strands were not part of that trial.
  • The old ogapmath.com site now redirects to BW Walch (checked Oct 25, 2026).

What a teacher gets

OGAP is a formative assessment system used alongside a school's existing math curriculum. Teachers learn research-based progressions of student thinking in four strands: additive reasoning (K–2), multiplicative reasoning (3–5), fractional reasoning (3–6) and proportional reasoning (6–8). The system includes item banks and frameworks, and trains teachers to analyze student work to recognize strategies and misconceptions and guide instruction. CPRE describes the package as teacher professional development, classroom resources, and school routines and supports.

It is delivered to districts; we found no individual-teacher purchase option. Evidence for ESSA describes each strand as four onsite professional learning days for a cohort of about 24 educators plus two onsite follow-up coaching days, with materials given to participants in the sessions. A CPRE randomized study in 61 Philadelphia-area schools reported that OGAP improved teacher knowledge and student learning in grades 3–5 (Supovitz, Ebby, Remillard and Nathenson, 2018). Several OGAP authors have also published Routledge books on fractions, multiplication and division, addition and subtraction, and ratios. We did not see the OGAP frameworks or item banks.

How these awards connect to this product

OGAP was developed through the Vermont Mathematics Partnership (VMP), which a 2009 MSP poster says was funded by NSF and the U.S. Department of Education; NSF award 0227057 is the VMP Math and Science Partnership award (PI Gross). That award's record does not name OGAP, so its share in OGAP's development is inferred. Award 1316527 (Penn, PI Supovitz) names OGAP and funded professional development and a randomized trial; its outcomes report says the project ran in 2014-2016 in two urban districts in the Philadelphia area, and the 1621333 outcomes report says that trial was also funded by NSF Award 1316527. 1621333 (Penn, PI Supovitz) builds on CPRE's NSF-funded OGAP experiment and adds systemic supports. 1620888 (Penn, PI Ebby) extended OGAP to grades K-2; CPRE says it produced the OGAP Additive Reasoning Framework and an item bank. Relation 'Funded part of this product', since sources do not show NSF funding the whole system: 1620888 funded design and development of the K-2 additive reasoning framework and item bank, and 1316527 funded OGAP PD and a randomized trial; NSF's role in developing the other strands through 0227057 is inferred. Marjorie Petit is listed as a developer (VMP and Marge Petit Consulting), not as a PI on any listed award.
