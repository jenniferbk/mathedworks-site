Before you use it

  • It is a paid program sold through McGraw Hill sales representatives. The 2020 catalog lists a grade 3 Comprehensive Student Material Set at $43.11 per student and each grade 3 Teacher Lesson Guide volume at $81.93; manipulative kits are sold separately, and we did not check current prices.
  • Digital components run through McGraw Hill's ConnectED platform. The catalog offers an All-Digital package or a Comprehensive set delivered both online and in print.
  • Grade 3 Home Links are free for families on the University of Chicago site, with no login, in English and Spanish; 11 of the 14 in Unit 1 have selected answers (we checked only that unit). The Home Link we read asks that it come back to school the next day.
  • Editions differ: the third edition (2007) had a 2012 Common Core update, and the fourth edition is separate. Materials are sold in English and Spanish.
  • Time per lesson is not stated in the sources we checked.

Students make a broken-line graph of the average number of days with precipitation in Omaha, then read and interpret two broken-line graphs on the same axes. For practice they find data landmarks by playing Landmark Shark.

You need: Math Journal 1 pages 18 and 19, a straightedge, and number cards for Landmark Shark.

What a teacher gets

Everyday Mathematics is a comprehensive PreK–6 core program written by the University of Chicago School Mathematics Project and published by McGraw Hill. The developers report it is used in about 220,000 US classrooms a year. The fourth edition (2020 catalog) was built for the Common Core State Standards.

Each grade from K to 6 has a two-volume Teacher's Lesson Guide, an Assessment Handbook with unit and interim assessments and record sheets, reproducible Math Masters, Activity Cards (over 100 per grade), and Minute Math quick activities. Students work in consumable Math Journals (grades 1–6) with daily Math Boxes for ongoing practice, use a reference book and take home a Home Link for each lesson. The developers describe lessons that mix whole-class instruction with small-group, partner or individual work, and lots of games and fact practice. Grade 3, Unit 1, for example, runs 14 lessons, including an open-response and reengagement lesson and a progress check. A ConnectED online teacher center carries the lessons, ePresentations, assessment and reporting tools.

How these awards connect to this product

No NSF award funded the fourth edition as far as our sources show: no Everyday Mathematics or McGraw Hill page we saved names an NSF award, and no source ties any award to the fourth edition (this is our reading, not a statement from the developers). The links come from the NSF records. 9252984 (Max Bell, University of Chicago, 1992-1999) funded Everyday Mathematics 4-6, the original grades 4-6 curriculum built on the K-3 program; it is the closest tie. 8550472 (Paul Sally, 1986-1990) supported the elementary (K-6) component of UCSMP's K-12 program (Amoco gave initial funding and Carnegie funded the secondary component); its record never uses the name Everyday Mathematics, so that link is inferred. 1109595 (PI Amy Cassata, co-PI Jeanne Century) funded a study of how Everyday Mathematics is implemented and sustained; it is research on the program, not development, and its researchers are not curriculum authors. Two awards titled "Curriculum Implementation through Staff Development" (9454360, Bell; 9596283, Sconiers) were left out: 9596283 has no abstract and the abstract on 9454360's record describes a different award (9454341, a physical science project).
