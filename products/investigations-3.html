Investigations 3 in Number, Data, and Space

A complete K–5 math curriculum from TERC, published by Savvas, with eight units per grade built around student thinking and discussion.

  • Grades K–5
  • Paid
  • No devices needed
  • Full curriculum, Teacher guide

Get the materialsinvestigations.terc.edu

Before you use it

  • Paid curriculum sold by Savvas; neither TERC nor the Savvas home page we captured posts prices. Requests for samples, demos and ordering go through Savvas.
  • Each classroom needs the grade's manipulative kit and card kits, and each student a Student Activity Book. For grade 4 the kit lists items such as 800 color tiles, 1000 snap cubes and 900 Power Polygons for a class of 28.
  • Technology is optional: TERC says the curriculum can be taught without technology, fully digitally or blended. Cards and games are also on Savvas Realize.
  • The 3rd edition (published 2016) is aligned to the Common Core. TERC's site no longer supports the first edition; see TERC's comparison pages for what changed from the second.
  • TERC offers PD for the curriculum (two-day online workshops, online courses and workshops in districts).

What a teacher gets

Investigations 3 is TERC's K–5 mathematics curriculum, published by Savvas Learning Company (formerly Pearson). Each grade has eight curriculum units, about 140 sessions per grade (132 in grade 4), an Implementation Guide and a Content Guide, with a Spanish Companion and a grade-level Assessment Sourcebook also available. It can be taught on paper, digitally, or blended.

TERC posts a free sample session for each grade. In the grade 4 sample we read (Unit 6, Session 4.2, fractions), the session opens with Ten-Minute Math, then a whole-class discussion of student strategies, a Math Workshop with two activity choices, and Daily Practice and Homework, with Common Core standards listed and "Students might say" examples for the teacher. TERC also posts unit summaries, a scope and sequence, Common Core correlations and materials lists for each grade, and comparisons with the second edition. Other sessions were not checked.

How these awards connect to this product

TERC's history page says NSF funded the first edition (developed 1990-1998, PI Susan Jo Russell) and, with TERC and Pearson Scott Foresman, the second edition (field-tested 2001-2004, published 2007). These match awards 9050210 (1990-2000) and 0095450 (2001-2007, a revision focused on algebra and assessment). The third edition sold today was developed 2011-2016 with funding from TERC and Pearson, not NSF; TERC says it is "based in part on work supported by the National Science Foundation" and based in large part on the second edition.
