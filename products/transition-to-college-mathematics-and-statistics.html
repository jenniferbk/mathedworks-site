Transition to College Mathematics and Statistics (TCMS)

A free fourth-year high-school course in statistics, functions, financial math, counting, informatics and voting, for college-bound students who won't take calculus.

  • Grades 9–12
  • Free, no login
  • Specific software
  • Full curriculum, Teacher guide

Get the materialsinfinitemath.com

Before you use it

  • The materials are free: the authors permit free public use, and commercial use needs their permission. The files were ported from the last published edition, and the authors warn of possible typos and design glitches.
  • The teacher's guide points to McGraw-Hill's ConnectED for the Unit Resource Masters, which hold assessments and the College Readiness Assessment sets. We found lesson quizzes inside the free teacher's guide file but did not check whether every resource master is there.
  • Technology is assumed: the course uses TCMS-Tools (Java-based) and graphing calculators. The TCMS-Tools address printed in the guide returns 404 (checked Oct 25, 2026), so plan on other spreadsheet, CAS, statistics and geometry software.
  • Students need a full algebra-geometry-algebra II sequence or three years of integrated mathematics first. Lessons run long: in Unit 1, Lessons 1–3 are paced at 9, 10 and 8 days, including assessment, and the Looking Back lesson at 2 days. The guide advises keeping the units in textbook order, since review tasks are designed for it.
  • The guide cites the Common Core State Standards and NCTM's Principles to Actions (2014); no publication date appears in the text we saved.

Try one lesson

Unit 1, Lesson 1, Investigation 1: Summarizing and Displaying the Risk drive.google.com

  • Fourth-year high school course
  • Interpreting Categorical Data
  • Time not stated

Students compute absolute risk from real data, such as failing rates for students who sit in the back or front of a class, compare stacked and grouped bar graphs of smoking and lung cancer data, then build two-way tables and bar graphs for leopard attacks and a survey, and pie charts for the survey.

The link opens Google Drive's page for TCMS SE.pdf, the full student edition; this investigation is on pages 3-8. The Teacher's Edition PDF on the same site has the teacher notes and answers.

What a teacher gets

Transition to College Mathematics and Statistics is a problem-based fourth-year course for students whose college programs do not require calculus, such as business, the social and life sciences, and many teacher preparation programs. It assumes students have finished algebra, geometry and algebra II, or a three-year integrated sequence such as Core-Plus Mathematics Courses 1-3, and it was field-tested in schools of both kinds.

The course has eight largely self-contained units: Interpreting Categorical Data, Functions Modeling Change, Counting Methods, Mathematics of Financial Decision-Making, Binomial Distributions and Statistical Inference, Informatics, Spatial Visualization and Representations, and Mathematics of Democratic Decision-Making. Teachers can reorder units, for example teaching the voting unit during an election. Lessons follow the Core-Plus pattern of collaborative investigations followed by teacher-led whole-class summaries. The teacher's guide has unit planning guides with objectives, homework assignments, resources and suggested pacing in days, and the guide file includes lesson quizzes. Student and teacher editions are free PDFs on the Core-Plus authors' site.
