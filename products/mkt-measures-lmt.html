Mathematical Knowledge for Teaching (MKT) measures, K-8 (teacher-facing instrument)

Secure survey forms measuring K–8 teachers' mathematical knowledge for teaching, for PD evaluation with groups; requires an application, paid training and conditions of use.

  • Grades K–8
  • Agreement or training required
  • Internet
  • Item bank, Teacher instrument

Get the materialssites.marsal.umich.edu

Before you use it

  • Access requires an application describing your background and purposes, $150 for online training and $500 per year to use the measures (fee waivers for those who qualify), and agreement to conditions of use. LMT releases the instruments only to trained users; allow three to six months.
  • Don't use them to judge individual teachers. LMT says the measures cannot be used to evaluate teachers for tenure, pay, hiring or any other high-stakes decision.
  • You'll need a group. LMT says about 60 teachers per group is usually adequate, 12–25 items are needed per scale, and teachers answer roughly one item a minute. The measures suit sustained PD aimed at broad gains in knowledge, not a 2–3 hour workshop.
  • Items were piloted with K–8 teachers; LMT notes that the joint AERA/APA Standards recommend re-piloting before use with other groups, and its technical summary says the geometry items may not fit K–2 teachers well.
  • The technical information we could read is a summary dated 2004–05, covering pilots from 2001 to 2004. It says the K–5 forms mostly cannot be used with middle-school teachers (ceiling effects), the knowledge-of-students-and-content scales are not reliable enough to detect moderate effects in mid-sized groups, and the items miss early-grade addition and subtraction. Ask LMT which forms it offers now and their current reliabilities.

Try one lesson

Mathematical Knowledge for Teaching (MKT) Measures: Mathematics Released Items, 2008 web.archive.org

  • Elementary and middle school
  • Mathematical knowledge for teaching

Teachers work through released survey items as open-ended prompts, for example judging whether three students' written methods for multiplying 35 by 25 would work for any two whole numbers, or whether 0 is an even number. The set has elementary content, knowledge of students and content, and middle school content items.

These are released items, not the secure measures: the developers ask you to request permission before any use and not to score teachers with them, and they allow use in professional development as open-ended prompts.

What a teacher gets

The LMT measures are survey items built around the mathematics teachers use in class: explaining a rule or procedure, judging an unusual solution method, choosing a definition that is accurate and usable with students, assessing student work. Piloted scales cover number and operations and patterns, functions and algebra (elementary and grades 6–8) and geometry (grades 3–8), plus a number and operations scale on knowledge of students and content. Forms within a pilot year were built to be parallel, so a group can take different forms before and after PD, but the technical summary says the 2004 forms, 2002 knowledge-of-students forms and middle-school items were not yet equated. Users can also build forms from the item pool.

The measures compare groups of teachers or track one group over time, as in a PD evaluation, not individuals. Users complete three online training modules of about 30–40 minutes each, then give paper forms or use the online Teacher Knowledge Assessment System, which scores the data. A booklet of released items can be used, with permission, as open-ended PD prompts; they cannot be scored.

How these awards connect to this product

The LMT home page credits NSF Grant No. 0335411. The 2008 released-items booklet credits measures development to NSF grants REC-9979873, REC-0207649, EHR-0233456 and EHR-0335411, and also by a subcontract to CPRE on a Department of Education, Office of Educational Research and Improvement (OERI) award, #R308A960003. No source says which items or forms each funder paid for, so we use "Funded part of this product." In the NSF API, 9979873 is an unrelated chemical engineering award at Purdue, so it is not listed; we could not identify the intended number. 0535816 (evaluation module development and dissemination) and 0634306 (computerized adaptive testing, describing LMT as NSF-funded) are listed because their records describe these measures. 1620914 (2016, Hill at Harvard) funded a refresh of the item pool and a move of the assessment system to Qualtrics; the LMT pages we saved do not say whether those new items are in the forms offered today. Shortlisted award 0126237 was left off because its record concerns the theory of mathematical knowledge for teaching and does not mention the measures.
