AC2inG: Animated Contrasting Cases in Geometry

Twenty-three free animated worked-example pairs for grade 8 geometry, in which students compare two fictional students' solutions, with discussion questions and a teacher guide.

Before you use it

  • Free, with no login: the animations run in a web browser at acinggeometry.org, so pairs or small groups need a device and internet. Paper-based versions are posted on the NC State site.
  • These supplement your instruction rather than replace it. Teach the listed prerequisite knowledge first, allow about 15–20 minutes per WEP plus discussion, and use no more than two WEPs a day alongside other instruction and practice.
  • Built for grade 8 geometry (Common Core 8.G standards). The developers say the WEPs fit best in grade 8 or with students learning that content; the project page mentions grades 7 and 8.
  • Print the discussion questions: they appear on screen after each pair, but the developers recommend paper copies. The NC State teacher-materials page posts one printable discussion-question PDF per unit.

What a teacher gets

AC2inG is a free set of supplemental geometry activities built on comparison. Each worked example pair (WEP) shows two fictional students solving a geometry problem. A self-paced animation presents each solution step by step; the two then appear side by side so students can compare them line by line, answer discussion questions and read a summary page of key takeaways. The 23 WEPs fall into four units: Angles (6), Transformations (6), Pythagorean Theorem (6) and Volume (5). Each WEP has one of four types: why does it work?, how do they differ?, which is better?, or which is correct? (one correct and one incorrect method).

The Teacher Resource Guide suggests teaching prerequisite knowledge first, then giving pairs or small groups about 15–20 minutes per WEP and its discussion questions, followed by a whole-class discussion. Each unit guide gives the unit's grade 8 Common Core geometry standards and, for each WEP, its key takeaway, prerequisite knowledge and vocabulary. The project's NC State site adds printable discussion-question pages and paper-based versions of the activities.
