Before you use it

  • You can view the tasks online at no cost, but only some are free to print. The home page excludes the primary and elementary tasks (now published by Corwin) and the transition and middle school tasks (now published by Teachers College Press) from its classroom duplication grant, and a note placed right after the Teachers College Press sentence says "these tasks" may be viewed as PDFs but not copied or printed; it is not clear whether that note also covers the Corwin tasks.
  • Printed packets and CD-ROMs are sold by mail with prepayment by check or purchase order only. The CD-ROM with a paper task browser (BA 2-00) is listed at $75.00 plus shipping and the CD-ROM and Index alone (BA 3-00) at $15.00; the CD excludes the tasks now published by Corwin and Teachers College Press, and because the order form is undated and the site was last updated in 2015, prices may be stale.
  • Plan on Microsoft Word for some tasks and rubrics (the high school and middle school samples we checked were Word documents, not PDFs). The MCAPS scoring spreadsheet needs Microsoft Excel or a compatible spreadsheet; its installer is offered for Windows 95/98/2000/Me and Windows 3.1, and the Mac version only on floppy disk.
  • The Corwin book of primary and elementary tasks (2007) is for grades PreK–4, and the Teachers College Press link on the site no longer resolves (checked Oct 25, 2026). A Spanish-language version of the elementary tasks, with English solutions and rubrics, is sold as a printed packet; the free online tasks we saw are in English.
  • Materials date from 1995–2003 and the site was last updated in 2015. We did not check which standards the tasks cite (the Corwin book describes its tasks as linked to NCTM standards), and time per task is not stated on the task pages we checked.

What a teacher gets

The Balanced Assessment in Mathematics Program ran at the Harvard Graduate School of Education from 1993 to 2003 and wrote a library of more than 300 assessment tasks for kindergarten through grade 12. The library stays online (the site footer now carries a Concord Consortium copyright), and teachers can use it in their classrooms at no cost. The site indexes the tasks by name and by number; its publications page groups them into printed packets (primary, elementary, transition, middle school and several high school packets).

Each task page links the student task and a separate solution and scoring rubric, and gives the grade level, content area and a 0–4 weight for each of four processes: modeling/formulating, transforming/manipulating, inferring/drawing conclusions and communicating. In the elementary task we read (Make a Map, grade 3–5), students read, correct and draw arrow diagrams of relationships; its rubric gives worked answers and partial- and full-level descriptions for each process. The site also offers the project's 1995 report on its assessment approach and its MCAPS "objects x actions" scoring system.

How these awards connect to this product

The task library's home page says its materials were developed under NSF grants MDR-9252902 and ESI-9726403, as subcontracts from the University of California at Berkeley and Michigan State University; the sample task and rubric checked carry the line "Supported by NSF Grant MDR-9252902". In the NSF API, 9252902 is Schoenfeld's Balanced Assessment for the Mathematics Curriculum award at Berkeley and 9726403 is Wilcox's MARS award at Michigan State. Harvard's director, Judah Schwartz, is named on the site, not on either NSF record. Other Balanced Assessment and MARS awards in this group are not named on the site and are not listed here. The NSF abstract for 9726403 describes MARS, a center giving states and districts technical help and professional development on assessment; it does not describe writing tasks. Its tie to the library is the site's credit line only.
