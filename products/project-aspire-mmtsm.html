A free 44-item written instrument showing PD leaders and teacher educators how a group of secondary teachers understands function, rate and proportion; scoring needs rubrics and training.

Before you use it

  • The form and animation video download free from the Aspire site, but most pages of the form say reproduction needs written permission. The full scoring rubrics aren't posted: the Aspire page asks you to contact Cameron Byerley through its email link to use the MMTsm and its rubrics. Cameron is one of the two math educators behind this index. Abbreviated rubrics for three items are in the 2023 article's appendix.
  • Plan for scorer training. The Aspire page says correct use of the rubrics requires training; the article says the rubrics were built for scorers with undergraduate mathematics and a few days of training. No training schedule or fee is posted; ask when you write.
  • It is for coaches, PD leaders, teacher educators and teacher teams looking at a group, not for judging one teacher. The developers say it is not meant for evaluating or promoting in-service teachers or grading preservice teachers.
  • You'll need two hours, a projector for the animation section, and pens: teachers write in ink so you can see first thoughts they later cross out.
  • The form pages are dated 2014 and 2016. A Korean version is available on request.

What a teacher gets

The MMTsm is a two-hour written instrument for groups of secondary mathematics teachers or future teachers. Project Aspire designed it for professional development and teacher education leaders to see which meanings for school mathematics are common in a group, for planning workshops and courses. Form 12 has 44 items. It opens with an 18-minute animation section, shown on a projected screen, in which teachers answer questions about animated items such as Average Speed, Similar Figures and Falling Balls; the rest are written items in the answer packet. Items cover function, function notation, rate of change, proportionality, frames of reference, measurement, covariation and structure sense. The associative-property item, for example, asks teachers to demonstrate as if to students, and the instructions say many items have no correct answer.

The Aspire site offers the form, a version with item identifiers, and the animation video. Each item has a scoring rubric. A 2023 article in Mathematics Teacher Educator prints abbreviated rubrics for three structure items with sample teacher responses; the full rubrics come from the authors.

Who made it

Project Aspire, Arizona State University and UC Berkeley. Developed by Patrick W. Thompson.

How these awards connect to this product

The MMTsm form credits NSF Grant No. MSP-1050595, and the 2023 Mathematics Teacher Educator article by Aspire team members says Project Aspire (NSF MSP-1050595) produced the MMTsm and its rubrics. The NSF record describes the project's teacher instrument as MKTsm (mathematical knowledge for teaching secondary mathematics) alongside a second instrument (IQAsm) for rating secondary instruction; no saved source says MKTsm and MMTsm are the same instrument under two names, but both the form and the article credit this award. This card covers only the teacher instrument.

Drafting notes and processing log

The Aspire page spells the contact's name "Byerly"; the article's author list and the project's records spell it Byerley, which the card uses.

The Mathematics Teacher Educator article (Musgrave, Byerley, Hatfield, Joshua & Yoon, 2023, vol. 12 no. 1, pp. 6-23) calls the MMTsm a 46-item instrument; Form 12 on the Aspire site says 44 items. The card uses the form.

The article says the complete MMTsm is at tinyurl.com/MMTsmDocuments, which redirects to a ResearchGate page ("Full MMTsm Assessment Related Documents"). ResearchGate blocked our fetcher; Jennifer Kleiman downloaded the file on 2026-10-30 (MMTsm.FinalForm12.forresearchers.pdf, 53 pages, kept in private/). It is Form 12 with item identifiers, the same 48 identifiers as the Aspire site's item-identifier PDF, plus a one-page demographic form. It has no rubrics or scoring guidance, says 44 items, and carries the same written-permission notice, so it changes nothing on the card. The article's 46-item count remains unexplained.

Cameron Byerley, the contact named on the Aspire page, is a co-author of the 2023 Mathematics Teacher Educator article and one of the two math educators who set this index's goal and inclusion bar.
