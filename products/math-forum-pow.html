Before you use it

  • You need a paid NCTM membership to open the problems: every problem page we opened (two) showed "Members-Only Access." NCTM's FY26 membership application lists Essential membership at $99 a year and Premium at $157, with lower introductory and student rates, and says that pricing was valid through May 31, 2026, so check NCTM for current dues.
  • We could read only the public listing, so we can't tell you what supporting materials (solutions, hints, rubrics) come with each problem today.
  • We can't date the problems: a 1997 NSF abstract names the Math Forum's Geometry Problem of the Week, a 2016 archive shows weekly problems on "The Math Forum at NCTM," and NCTM linked Problems of the Week resources by 2020. The listing gives no dates, so check each problem's context before you use it.
  • Grade fit varies by problem; the listings we read state grade ranges from Grades K-1 to Grades 11-12.

Try one lesson

What a teacher gets

Problems of the Week began at the Math Forum, which NSF supported, and is now a library on NCTM's website. In a 2016 archive of the Math Forum at NCTM, weekly problems were posted in strands such as Primary, Math Fundamentals, Pre-Algebra, Algebra, Geometry, and Trig & Calculus, most with a link to the latest solution.

NCTM's public listing shows 1,736 problems that you can filter by grade band (PreK to 2nd, 3rd to 5th, 6th to 8th, High School, Higher Education) and by subject, such as number sense, probability and derivatives. The listings we could read (the first 20 shown on the page) give a title, a grade range and a one-sentence prompt; for example, A Box of Cubes (grades 3-5) asks how many 1-inch cubes fit in a 10 by 6 by 3 inch box. NCTM says members have access to the problems and supporting materials. Every problem page we opened is members-only. NCTM also posts a public example that downloads without a login, Teresa's Tiles, describing how one teacher presented a problem to an eighth-grade class.

How these awards connect to this product

The abstract of NSF award 1222355 (Jason Silverman, Drexel University, 2012-2018) describes the project as "building on the success of the NSF-supported Math Forum's Problem of the Week program." Award 9618223, "The Math Forum" (Eugene A Klotz, Swarthmore College, 1997-2000), cites the success of "the Geometry Problem of the Week" as one of the Forum's interactive projects. Both are confirmed in the NSF API. No source says which of the problems now on NCTM's site were written under NSF awards, and NCTM, not the Math Forum, now publishes and maintains the library (an archived 2020 NCTM page says NCTM would keep "many of the most popular parts of the Math Forum content accessible"). We therefore use "Connection inferred": NSF supported the Math Forum and its Problem of the Week program, but the sources do not tie any award to the library as NCTM publishes it today. For 1222355, the abstract describes teacher assessment and rubric work and doesn't mention writing problems. Other Math Forum awards (for example 0226284 and 0717732) also mention the Problems of the Week; we did not list them.

Who made it: no saved source names the people who wrote the Problems of the Week.
