CODAP (Common Online Data Analysis Platform)

A free, browser-based data analysis tool built for students, with drag-and-drop graphs, maps and linked tables, sample datasets and built-in tutorials.

  • Grades 3–12
  • Free, no login
  • Device per student, Internet
  • Framework or toolkit, Software

Get the materialscodap.concord.org

Before you use it

  • Free, with no account or login; it runs in the browser, so nothing needs installing. You need an internet-connected computer and a recent browser; the FAQ says CODAP was developed and tested mainly in Chrome.
  • Phones are too small to use it well, and some features are not yet available on tablets. On an iPad, hold a little longer than expected before dragging.
  • Save your work: saving to Google Drive requires a Google account connected to CODAP; otherwise download documents as .codap files. Only one person at a time can edit a shared document; the FAQ suggests passing it back and forth.
  • Offline use is limited: CODAP works offline only if loaded online first, then accepts only local CSV or TXT data, and embedded web pages, maps and plugins will not load.
  • There are no lessons or assessments in CODAP itself. Plan to use the sample documents or a partner curriculum, or to write your own tasks.

What a teacher gets

CODAP is a free, open-source web app for exploring data, geared toward middle and high school students and aimed at teachers and curriculum developers (the educators page says grades 5 to 14). Students drag attributes from a case table onto a graph, place latitude and longitude data on maps, and select points in one representation to see them highlighted in the others. Data can be dropped in as CSV files, pasted, typed or pulled in by plugins.

The app (version 3.1.2 on Oct 25, 2026) opens on a choice of a new document or examples: two Getting Started documents plus Mammals, Roller Coasters, Four Seals, Parachute Model, Markov Game, PhET Projectile Data Lab, Duck Pond and Map Data. Roller Coasters (157 U.S. coasters, 16 attributes) opens with an ESTEEM-made guide listing student steps, such as dragging Top Speed to the x-axis and Age Group to the y-axis to compare older and newer coasters. CODAP has no lessons or assessments of its own; the site links free curricula built on it, such as Bootstrap Data Science for grades 6–12.

How these awards connect to this product

Both awards are titled "Common Online Data Analysis Platform (CODAP)" and their abstracts say the team will develop and test CODAP, so the NSF records themselves tie them to this product. The first went to KCP Technologies in 2013 and the second to the Concord Consortium in 2014; the CODAP GitHub README says CODAP received NSF funding as a project in its own right in April 2014. Both abstracts describe a grades 9–12 science focus; the CODAP site now presents the tool for grades 5 and up across subjects. The README says development began in 2010 under KCP Technologies' NSF-supported Data Games project (awards 0918653 and 0918735 in the NSF API); those awards are not listed here because their records describe the data games, not CODAP. CODAP's About page credits NSF along with private funders without naming award numbers.
