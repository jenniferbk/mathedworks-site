Before you use it

  • The August 2026 library capture lists the lessons at $0.00, marked down from $20.00, and each course page has an Enroll link. Jennifer Kleiman, who checked the site on Oct 30, 2026, reports that you can get an account and many lessons are free.
  • The lesson courses sit on a Canvas catalog named "TLSW Test", so the links may move. lessonsketch.org answers automated visitors with a Cloudflare check, though it opens in a browser.
  • These are lesson representations to study, annotate and discuss with other teachers, not ready-to-teach plans or scripts.
  • The library is small (seven lessons in August 2026), mostly geometry and algebra for high school.

What a teacher gets

LessonSketch's lesson library holds representations of secondary mathematics lessons, drawn as storyboards with cartoon characters (and, for one lesson, animations) that you view, annotate and discuss with other teachers. The library page we saved lists seven lessons: Line Through Two Points (a temperature-conversion problem), Difference of Functions (two moving vehicles), the Midpoint of Hypotenuse and Tangent Segments theorems, and three problem-based lessons on longitude, the horizon and gerrymandering. Each lesson is a self-paced course on a Canvas catalog, and the course pages name high school mathematics teachers, teacher educators and students as the audience.

Three lessons come as storyboard pairs. For longitude and gerrymandering, New York educators designed and taught the first version in grade 11, and a University of Michigan team wrote the second to imagine the lesson going differently. For horizon, New York and Michigan educators made the first together, and the Michigan team's second shows how it could play out in a high school classroom. The pages present the storyboards as resources for exploring a lesson's possibilities, not as teaching scripts.

Who made it

LessonSketch. Developed by Patricio Herbst, Daniel Chazan and Amanda Brown.

How these awards connect to this product

No LessonSketch or catalog page we saved names an award number; the course pages list "National Science Foundation, James S. McDonnell Foundation" as sponsors. We tie awards through their NSF records only. 2201087 (Brown and Herbst, University of Michigan, 2022-2027) funds StoryCircles groups of teachers who design algebra and geometry lesson storyboards that teachers in the US and abroad annotate; three of the library's lessons say their storyboards came from StoryCircles. 1316241 (Chazan and Herbst, 2013-2020) set out to develop the LessonSketch.org platform and teacher education modules for preservice programs, and 1420102 (Herbst, 2014-2020) planned to make a simulation-based environment available at LessonSketch for teacher educators and candidates. Neither of those two modules or environments appears in the library we saw, so all three are listed as an inferred connection. 1316241's record names the LessonSketch.org platform (and 1420102's names LessonSketch); 2201087's record never mentions LessonSketch and ties in only through StoryCircles.

Drafting notes and processing log

We read the course descriptions on the Canvas catalog, not the storyboards, which sit behind enrollment; a free account to view one lesson is in the human-assist queue.

The library and enrollment details come partly from Jennifer Kleiman's human check on 2026-10-30, because lessonsketch.org shows our User-Agent a Cloudflare challenge. The library page itself is an August 2026 Wayback copy.

The catalog host is a test catalog by its name ("TLSW Test", tlsw-test.catalog.instructure.com); Jennifer reached it from a live lessonsketch.org lesson page, so it is what teachers get today.

The course pages name teacher educators as an audience but say nothing about methods courses or prospective teachers; the teacher-preparation fit rests on that audience line and on 1316241's preservice aims. In scope either way, since the pages also name high school teachers.

Granularity is Single task because each course holds one lesson's representations. The primary_user tag 'PD facilitator or coach' stands in for teacher educators; the schema has no teacher-educator value yet.

The horizon and gerrymandering pages repeat the phrase "how the Longitude Lesson might unfold", a copy-over slip on the catalog; we did not quote it.

Earlier screens excluded LessonSketch for audience and for the bot wall (eval/shortlists, how-we-chose.md).

Grade band is 9-12 only: the course pages name high school teachers, and grade 11 courses appear in two lessons. The archived library's '7 to 9' grade filter (4 lessons) straddles bands, so 6-8 was dropped.
