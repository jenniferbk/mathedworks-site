Before you use it

  • The official validated tests are paid, and no price is posted. ThinkData Ed's page for students and parents says each test needs a purchased access code and that a breakdown report by category costs $10 more. Teachers are directed to email info@thinkdataed.org for discount codes to share with students.
  • The sample items are free to browse, and building your own test from them takes a free account. The online versions run in a web browser; the LOCUS site says paper versions are available by contacting Tim Jacobbe.
  • Check which form you'll get: the LOCUS site lists 23-, 30- and 50-question online versions, but ThinkData Ed's student and parent page says LOCUS has 30 multiple-choice questions and takes 60 minutes.
  • Grade fit: the sample items cite Common Core standards (for example 6.SP.1). The paper Beginning/Intermediate form is listed for grades 6–12 and Intermediate/Advanced for grades 10–12.
  • ThinkData Ed markets LOCUS as a predictor of AP Statistics scores; we have not seen a published study of that claim.

What a teacher gets

LOCUS measures conceptual understanding of statistics across the four GAISE stages of statistical problem solving: formulating questions, collecting data, analyzing data and interpreting results. The LOCUS site lists three official online versions, all multiple choice with automatic reports and each with two equated forms for pre- and post-testing: Beginning/Intermediate (23 questions), Intermediate/Advanced (30) and Beginning/Intermediate/Advanced (50), taking about 45–60 minutes. The LOCUS site also describes paper versions with five constructed-response questions (90 minutes). The tests were piloted in 2013 and finalized in 2014.

What a teacher can use for free: a bank of sample items, which ThinkData Ed puts at 68 multiple-choice and 33 constructed-response items, browsable by grade (6, 7, 8, high school) or by problem-solving stage. The one item we opened showed the correct answer, commentary on the reasoning and the Common Core standards it addresses. With a free account you can assemble your own test from these sample items; those custom tests are not the official validated forms.

How these awards connect to this product

1118168 (University of Florida, Jacobbe as PI, from September 2011) is titled LOCUS and funded development of two GAISE-based statistics assessments for grades 6-12 with multiple-choice and constructed-response items. Its start matches the project timeline on the LOCUS site. Its reported publications include 'Lessons from the LOCUS assessments' articles and an Amstat News piece titled 'NSF funds research in statistics education: LOCUS'. The tie rests on the award record; the current LOCUS and ThinkData Ed pages we saved do not mention NSF. The award abstract says the assessments would be made available as open source to teachers through a website; today the official forms need purchased access codes.
