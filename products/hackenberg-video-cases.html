A Case of Differentiating Instruction for Prospective Secondary Teachers (Quantifying Speed video case)

A free online video case on tiering instruction, built around days 9–13 of a 7th-grade unit, when students worked on speed, with video and audio clips, a transcript and discussion questions.

Before you use it

  • It's free: no login was needed to open the course or download its clips and PDFs (checked Oct 30, 2026). It sits in a Canvas course labeled "Sandbox," and we found no statement about how long it will stay up, so check the link before you plan around it.
  • The course page needs JavaScript turned on. Use a laptop, desktop or tablet for the race applet; the case notes that it is often glitchy on a phone.
  • It's grade 7 ratio work on speed. The class used the CMP3 unit Comparing and Scaling some of the time; this investigation was modeled on Joanne Lobato and colleagues' online proportional reasoning unit, with Janet Bowers's race app. It is a case to study, written for prospective teachers, not a lesson set.
  • The students used an older version of the applet with a blue car; the version linked in the case has a silver car, so the clips don't match the screen exactly. Clip lengths and how long the case takes are not stated.
  • No reuse license is stated, and the course's Canvas record lists its license as "private." Ask the authors before copying the clips into your own course site.

What a teacher gets

This case follows a 7th-grade class through the start of an investigation of speed (days 9–13 of a 26-day ratio unit that Amy Hackenberg co-taught with the classroom teacher). Students used a race applet to make one car go slower than, then the same speed as, another. To tier the work, the team gave groups different distance and time numbers based on each student's fractions knowledge.

The case has five parts. You first work the tasks yourself in the applet and read an introduction to the class and the tiering plan. Then you watch three video clips of Sara and Lisa, two of Emily's group, two audio clips of Joanna's group, and a whole-class discussion, answering questions about student thinking and teacher moves after each. Parts 2–5 each have a PDF of their questions, and one transcript covers the clips. The case ends with takeaway messages about tiered tasks. The team built it for prospective teachers and presented it at AMTE in February 2020, with shorter clips for teacher educators.

Who made it

IDR²eAM (Investigating Differentiated Instruction and Relationships between Rational Number Knowledge and Algebraic Reasoning in Middle School). Developed by Amy J. Hackenberg.

Its project outcomes report says the team "developed a case study and an article for teachers about how to use different number choices," and the project's Accomplishments page lists this video case among its 2018–2020 work.
