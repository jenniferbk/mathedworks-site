Instructional Nudges (Practice-Driven Professional Development)

A library of 32 "instructional nudges", small teaching moves each with a PDF and most with short videos, from an NSF-funded professional development project for algebra teachers.

  • Free, no login

Before you use it

  • We found no price. The nudge pages and their PDFs opened for us without an account.
  • These are small moves to add to lessons you already teach, not lessons or a curriculum. The examples in the PDFs are brief and span topics, from what numbers multiply to 12 (Reversal) to the area of a trapezoid (Leave a Trace).
  • The project's professional development was for algebra teachers, but the nudge PDFs we read state no grade level.
  • Ten of the 32 nudges have a PDF but no video, and the videos play on the web pages, so you'll need internet access. Eighteen nudge pages link a second PDF; on the four we read it is labeled "Classic version (with Level Ups)." On Super Wrong, Kinda Right, a reader reported in October 2026 that a template link didn't work, and the site replied that the link was broken. We don't know whether it has been fixed.
  • The site states no license for reuse. The four nudge pages we read each give a suggested citation.

This is a teaching move, not a lesson. After students have solved typical problems, you give them an answer and ask them to create a problem for it; the PDF's example asks what numbers can be multiplied to get 12. The page has two short introduction videos, a demonstration video and a one-page PDF.

The PDF cautions that one reversal usually goes a long way. The site states no license; the page gives a suggested citation.

What a teacher gets

Instructional Nudges is an online library from the Practice-Driven Professional Development project, led by Zandra de Araujo (University of Florida), Samuel Otten (University of Missouri at Columbia) and Amber Candela (University of Missouri at St. Louis). The project calls a nudge a small, high-uptake teaching action meant to fit into how you already teach. Its professional development was built for algebra teachers.

The library lists 32 nudges, each on its own page with a PDF version. The four current PDFs we read each fit on one page: the move ("Try it"), reasons to try it, an illustrated example, teacher notes, a caution and a reference, three of them to journal articles. In Reversal, students who have solved typical problems get an answer and create a problem for it. In First Attempt, students try a problem before you demonstrate a method. Twenty-two nudge pages have short videos introducing the nudge, and 16 of those include a demonstration. You can browse them by lesson segment, from starting a lesson to wrapping up, or by goal, such as attending to student thinking.

How these awards connect to this product

The project home page says "This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Grants #2101508 and #2206774." Both are confirmed in the NSF API under the same title, Practice-Driven Professional Development for Algebra Teachers, with Zandra U de Araujo as PI, in the Discovery Research K-12 program: 2101508 at the University of Missouri-Columbia (07/01/2021 to 12/31/2021, with Samuel J Otten and Amber Candela as co-PIs) and 2206774 at the University of Florida (12/01/2021 to 09/30/2026). The NSF project outcomes report for 2206774 says the project developed "18 instructional nudges delivered through one-page PDFs, short example videos, and web-based resources." The library now lists 32 nudges. The awards funded the 18 nudges and the online professional learning system the report describes; no source says whether the other 14 nudges were built under these awards, though the site credits only these two grants, so we use "Funded part of this product."

Drafting notes and processing log

What we read: the home, about, team, publications and presentations pages; the library index and its by-goal and by-lesson-segment pages; four nudge pages (Reversal, Leave a Trace, First Attempt, and "Super Wrong, Kinda Right") and their current PDFs, plus the classic PDFs for Reversal and First Attempt. We fetched all 32 nudge pages once to count videos and PDF links (nudge-survey-2026-10-29.txt) but did not read the other 28 PDFs or watch the videos. The text extractor dropped the video captions on the nudge pages, so we also saved each read page's full visible text.

The NSF outcomes report for 2206774 describes an online professional learning system that engaged 60 Algebra teachers from 14 states. We found and examined only the public nudge library; we did not look for or try to use that system. The same report describes gains for teachers and students, but it is the project's own report, not a published study, so the card makes no effectiveness claim.

Tags: the nudges are teaching moves, not tied to one math topic. We tag Expressions and equations because the project worked with algebra teachers. We tag grades 6-8 because the PI presented the nudges for middle school. No saved source states high school or a grade range for the nudges, so we don't tag 9-12, although the project worked with Algebra 1 teachers and presented on secondary mathematics teachers' choice of nudges. The presentations page also lists a talk on nudges for elementary mathematical reasoning. The nudge PDFs we read state no grade.

Our PME-NA scout (eval/shortlists/pmena_scout.json, 2026-10-25) set this project aside as "PD materials being field-tested; no public release found." That was a miss: the nudge library is public. The scout also grouped an earlier de Araujo award on flipped instruction (1721025) with this project; no source ties it to the nudges, so it is not listed.

Research list: filled by pipeline/fill_research.py from the papers reported to NSF and OpenAlex funding metadata. One entry is a Research Square preprint, not a peer-reviewed paper. The papers are about the project's professional development and algebra teaching; one, "Rate and Review," shares its title with a nudge in the library, but we did not read it, and none is listed as evidence that the nudges work.

A recommendation that prompted this card came from the project's PI, so the card claims no outside endorsement.
