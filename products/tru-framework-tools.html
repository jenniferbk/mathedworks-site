TRU Framework tools (Conversation and Observation Guides for mathematics)

Two PDF guides, downloadable without login, that help math teachers, coaches and administrators plan, observe and talk about lessons using TRU's five dimensions.

Before you use it

  • No price is shown and the PDFs download without an account. You may copy and share the guides for non-commercial purposes but not modify them.
  • The Observation Guide is labeled a draft ("Alpha Fifth Complete Draft"); the Conversation Guide is labeled "Release Version Beta" and calls itself a working document. Both are dated March 2, 2018, and the site links those 2018 PDFs.
  • Plan on ongoing, collaborative use over a term or a year, with time to plan, observe and debrief.
  • The first observation sheet's "look fors" are general across mathematics and are meant to be supplemented with specifics for the lesson being observed.
  • Neither guide names a grade range. The research behind them came from middle and high school algebra classrooms, and 1503454 and the related TRU+Lesson Study materials (on the SERP Institute site) are for high school math teachers.

Try one lesson

Teaching Mathematics for Robust Understanding mathnic.org

  • Middle school and high school
  • What makes a mathematically powerful classroom?
  • 90 minutes, with opportunities for expansion
  • Free, no login

Teachers watch three short classroom videos, starting with a TIMSS lesson on finding angles, and note what it is like to be a student in each class. The leader sorts their comments into five columns that become the TRU dimensions, then introduces tools including the TRU Math Conversation Guide.

You need: The workshop slides, a whiteboard or five flip charts with markers, and one copy of the session handouts per participant.

The materials are published under a Creative Commons BY-NC-SA license, so they may be copied and adapted for non-commercial use with attribution.

What a teacher gets

Teaching for Robust Understanding (TRU) describes classrooms along five dimensions: the mathematics, cognitive demand, equitable access to mathematics, agency, ownership and identity, and formative assessment. Its website offers two tools, downloadable without login, each in a mathematics-specific and a domain-general version.

The TRU Math Conversation Guide is a set of reflective questions for teacher teams, coaches and administrators. Each dimension gets core questions, a description, paired Planning and Reflecting prompts, and a list of things to think about. The authors ask users to pick questions like a menu around a long-term learning agenda, not to work through every one, and suggest uses from department vision-setting to peer observation, video study and individual reflection.

The TRU Observation Guide for Mathematics has one observation sheet per dimension: a description, student behaviors to look for, teacher actions that could support them, space for focal points and notes, and key goals. Teacher and observer agree on a focus before the lesson; the "look fors" are not a checklist.

How these awards connect to this product

The TRU Observation Guide for Mathematics and the truframework.org footer credit NSF award 1503454 (TRUmath and Lesson Study, Schoenfeld, Berkeley, 2015-2020); its NSF abstract describes building professional learning for high school mathematics departments on the TRUmath framework. The TRU Math Conversation Guide credits the Algebra Teaching Study, NSF grants DRL-0909815 (Schoenfeld) and DRL-0909851 (Floden, Michigan State). Those records' abstracts describe a study of Algebra 1 word-problem teaching; their project outcomes reports say the tools developed were a classroom observation scheme (TRU Math) and a student assessment for middle and high school algebra classrooms. So 1503454 is credited only for the Observation Guide and parts of the website; no source says it funded the Conversation Guide, which credits the two Algebra Teaching Study awards (and a Gates grant) instead. Because the two guides credit different NSF awards and both also credit Gates Foundation grants, we list NSF as having funded part of this product. Both guides also credit Gates Foundation grants (OPP53342 to the Mathematics Assessment Project; OPP1115160). The Balanced Assessment and MARS awards in this group are not credited in either guide. The MathNIC TRU workshop, which truframework.org links to, carries no NSF credit in the page or guide we saved: MathNIC's home page says the network that designed its tools convened in 2015 with support from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, and the workshop guide is © 2017 Math Assessment Resource Service, UC Berkeley.
