What a teacher gets

LEAP is an early-algebra program, one product per grade from kindergarten through grade 5, sold by Didax. Didax describes it as supplementing any math curriculum, and its brochure says to teach the lessons throughout the school year. Each grade has 18-20 lessons, which Didax and TERC describe as one hour each. The grades 3-5 lessons are organized around three big ideas: equivalence, expressions, equations (statements that two expressions are equivalent, such as 2 + 3 = 1 + 4) and inequalities; generalized arithmetic; and functional thinking. The grades 3–5 brochure says assessments come every four to five lessons.

We read the free Grade 3 sample, Lesson 3.1 on the equal sign. After an overview, objectives, a rationale for each task, vocabulary and materials come a whole-class Jumpstart, small-group Explore and Discuss and a whole-class Review and Discuss (15, 35 and 10 minutes in the brochure's Grade 3 lesson-at-a-glance). Teacher notes describe what operational and relational thinking sound like, common difficulties, and support for struggling learners. The brochure gives that three-part structure for every lesson; we saw only this one.

How these awards connect to this product

Listed awards built the lessons. TERC's project page credits NSF 1207945 for the work that developed the grades 3-5 progression, lesson sequences and assessments, and 1720129 for the grades K-2 progression prototype. 0918239 has the same PI (Blanton) and word-for-word the same abstract as 1207945 but a different awardee (UMass Dartmouth); we infer it is the same project at an earlier institution. TERC's page does not name it. NSF paid for part of the work: Didax says the curriculum was funded in part by NSF and the U.S. Department of Education, and TERC lists IES grants R305A140092 (a teacher-led efficacy study, grades 3-5) and R305A170378 (extending the progression into grades K-2). TERC also credits NSF awards that funded studies rather than lesson development, so they are not listed above: 1219605/1219606 (quasi-experimental study of the grades 3-5 intervention), 2404984 (2024-2028 randomized study of the K-2 intervention), 1550897 (retention study) and 1721192 (classroom observation instrument).
