Before you use it

  • The site posts no price and asks for no sign-up to read the activity pages, and the Graphing Taboo PDF downloaded without a login. The other eight activities open on Amplify Classroom (the older teacher.desmos.com links redirect there); the site doesn't say whether you need an Amplify account to run them with a class.
  • You'll need internet-connected devices for the eight online activities; the site doesn't say whether students work one per device or in pairs. Graphing Taboo needs printed card sets, one set per student in groups of four.
  • No teacher notes, grade levels, timings or standards are posted on the activity pages, so work through an activity yourself before class. Drone Delivery says students should complete Go Kart first.
  • The project is aimed at high school and undergraduate algebra and calculus. Several activities are calculus ideas (Pool Fill uses Riemann sums; Major Tom links acceleration to velocity and displacement, and Drone Delivery to velocity and position), so check fit for your course.
  • These are designs from a research project that runs to July 2028, and the activity titles carry version labels (Major Tom v13, Go Kart v11), so activities may change or move.

  • Function families, properties of functions, and transformations

In groups of four, each student gets a set of cards and describes a graph so teammates can find its function rule without saying any of the card's Taboo words. Cards cover linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, logarithmic and rational functions and conics.

You need: Printed card sets; pink cards are worth one point and purple cards two

The activity list calls it Function Taboo; its page heading is Graphing Taboo, and the page links the cards as a PDF (Taboo.pdf) on Google Drive. The page states no grade level. No price is stated; the PDF downloaded without a login.

What a teacher gets

Playful Mathematics is a small collection of nine activities from a University of Georgia and University of Idaho research project on making school mathematics more playful. The team designs activities for algebra and calculus around six principles, among them free exploration within constraints, authentic feedback, and letting the student act as designer.

Eight are online activities that open on Amplify Classroom, five of them through older Desmos links. In Major Tom, students control directional accelerations and see how they change velocity and displacement; Drone Delivery explores how acceleration, velocity and position are related; Go Kart relates a kart's position and velocity; Pizza Shop Pickup relates speed to distance and time; Pool Fill has students estimate the water poured into a pool from a table and an animation, using Riemann sums. In Tropical Algebra Balances and Transforming Geometry, students work out what unfamiliar operations do, and in Mystery Transformation they guess a hidden graph transformation. The ninth, Graphing Taboo, is a printable card game for groups of four about function families. Each activity page gives a short premise and a link.

How these awards connect to this product

The project site says "Our NSF-funded project, Characterizing and Fostering Playful Mathematics for Undergraduate and High School Learning" and "We are designing playful math activities for algebra and calculus." That title matches two collaborative NSF awards confirmed in the API: 2347820 (Amy B Ellis, University of Georgia Research Foundation) and 2347821 (Robert Ely, University of Idaho), both starting 08/15/2024 and running to 07/31/2028. The project's own research list shows playful-math work by the same team from 2022, before these awards began, and no source says which of the nine activities were built under the awards, so we use "Funded part of this product" rather than "Funded this product." Crossref lists the National Science Foundation and the University of Georgia as funders of the 2025 Journal of Mathematical Behavior article, without an award number.

Drafting notes and processing log

We read the Graphing Taboo card PDF in full. The other eight activities are online activities on Amplify Classroom (five reached through older Desmos links) that render only with JavaScript: our fetches of three of them (Tropical Algebra Balances, Major Tom, Go Kart) returned just the title, author and "Loading...", so the card describes them from the project's activity pages, not from the activities themselves. We did not open them in a browser.

The Google Drive view page for Taboo.pdf returned a sign-in shell to our fetcher; the direct-download URL returned the PDF (served as application/octet-stream), which we saved under private/raw and converted with pdftotext by hand because fetch_source.py only converts responses labeled as PDF.

Grade band: the activity pages state no grade levels. We tag 9-12 from the project and award title ("Undergraduate and High School Learning") and the award abstract ("secondary and undergraduate"). Several activities are calculus topics, so fit for a given high school course varies.

Math domains come from the activity descriptions: Expressions and equations (Tropical Algebra Balances: graphing and solving equations), Functions (Graphing Taboo, Mystery Transformation), Geometry (Transforming Geometry), Precalculus and calculus (Pool Fill, Major Tom, Go Kart, Drone Delivery). Pizza Shop Pickup (speed, distance and time) is not tagged separately.

Access is tagged "Not stated": the site posts no price or login requirement, and the Taboo PDF downloads without a login, but we did not test whether the Amplify Classroom activities can be run with a class without an Amplify account.

The research list keeps only the 2025 Journal of Mathematical Behavior article from the project's own /research page, the one paper whose funders we checked; the other nine publications and the presentations are reachable through research_home. The NSF records for 2347820 and 2347821 list no reported publications. The 2022-2023 papers predate the August 2024 awards; we do not claim NSF funded them. For the 2025 JMB article, Crossref lists NSF and the University of Georgia as funders without an award number; we did not see the article's acknowledgment text, so we don't know which NSF award, if any, it acknowledges.
