Before you use it

  • This is a college textbook. For a high school course, order the AP Edition through Perfection Learning, which posts no price; you'll need to ask for a quote.
  • Wiley's college pricing (Oct 25, 2026) ranges from a $54.00 150-day e-book rental to $215.95 for multi-term WileyPLUS access with a loose-leaf text.
  • Some 8th-edition features (pre-lecture videos with assignable questions, computer science labs) are in WileyPLUS, which costs extra. Wiley also lists adaptive assignments powered by Knewton and sells Knewton Alta with the e-book as a separate $75.95 option.
  • Chapters 12–21 of the Single and Multivariable text go beyond AP Calculus; the Single Variable AP Edition stops at chapter 11.
  • The 8th edition dates from 2020–21. NSF funded the Consortium's original course development from 1989 to 1998; the current edition is a commercial Wiley product.

Students read how a function, such as Boston's daily snowfall or a cricket's chirp rate as a function of temperature, can be shown by tables, graphs, formulas and words (the Rule of Four), then work exercises such as explaining what f(5) = 8 means for a city's population and matching graphs to equations.

This is Wiley's free excerpt of the college edition, not the AP Edition sold to high schools: the PDF is all of Chapter 1 (86 pages), and section 1.1 comes first with its exercises and problems; there is no lesson plan. The grades 9–12 band is the AP Edition's.

What a teacher gets

This is the textbook from the Calculus Consortium based at Harvard, organized by Deborah Hughes-Hallett and Andrew Gleason. Its signature is the Rule of Four: problems are viewed graphically, numerically, symbolically and verbally. The 8th edition has 21 chapters: chapters 1-11 cover single-variable calculus (functions and limits, the derivative, the definite integral, antiderivatives, series, differential equations), and chapters 12-21 cover multivariable and vector calculus.

For high schools, Perfection Learning sells an AP Edition for grades 9-12 and AP Calculus AB and BC, in a Single and Multivariable version and a Single Variable version whose chapters 1-11 are the same. It says the course is correlated to the College Board's Course and Exam Description. In the Chapter 1 excerpt we read, sections 1.1–1.9 end with exercises, problems and a Strengthen Your Understanding set; section 1.10 is an online resource. Wiley says the book needs no specific software and has been used with graphing calculators, graphing software and computer algebra systems. The current edition adds pre-lecture videos and active-learning worksheets for every section.

How these awards connect to this product

The link is the Calculus Consortium itself: Wiley names the Consortium as the book's source, and NSF's records show NSF funded the Consortium's course development. 8953923 (1989-1995) funded a core calculus curriculum by a consortium of eight institutions, including Chelmsford High School, built on a 'Rule of Three' (graphical, numerical, analytical). 9352905 (1994-1998) says the Consortium, with NSF funding, developed, tested and disseminated a single-variable course and was extending it to precalculus and second-year calculus. Both were undergraduate (DUE) awards. No source about the textbook mentions NSF, and no source says NSF funded the 8th edition, which Wiley publishes commercially. Not listed: 8813997 (a 1988 one-year planning grant), and 9252521 and 9455970, which no source ties to the textbook.
