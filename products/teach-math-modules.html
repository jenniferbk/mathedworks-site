TEACH Math learning modules for K-8 mathematics methods courses

Three free modules that teacher educators can use in mathematics methods courses to help prospective K-8 teachers connect children's mathematical thinking with their home and community knowledge.

Before you use it

  • These are teacher-preparation materials for the instructor of a mathematics methods course and the prospective K-8 teachers in it, not lessons for K-8 students. They assume each prospective teacher has a practicum (field-placement) classroom and a child there to interview and shadow, and they build in written course work: write-ups or in-class quick writes turned in to the methods instructor.
  • Free downloads with no login (two PDFs and one Word file); the site states no license beyond a request to cite the modules.
  • The Classroom Practices module depends on video cases it does not include, many from Annenberg's Teaching Math video library. The learner.org links the module gives now return 404 (checked Oct 25, 2026), so you will need to find the videos elsewhere or substitute your own.
  • Plan for time across a term: the community exploration can run over three weeks mostly in class or six weeks mostly outside class, and the case study module gives sample sequences for 10- and 15-week courses.
  • The materials are cited as 2015.

What a teacher gets

TEACH Math posts three modules for teacher educators who teach K-8 mathematics methods courses, built to help prospective teachers draw on both children's mathematical thinking and their home and community knowledge. The Community Mathematics Exploration module has three activities: a community walk near the field-placement school, design of a problem-based lesson or task that draws on the walk, and a final reflection. The Mathematics Learning Case Study module has prospective teachers interview one child, shadow that child for part or all of a day, conduct problem-solving interviews (whole-number and fraction protocols included), and write a synthesis. The Classroom Practices module has four repeatable activities: analyzing video cases through four lenses (teaching, learning, task, and power and participation), analyzing curriculum materials with a Curriculum Spaces Analysis Table, analyzing lesson plans, and analyzing observed or one's own teaching.

Each module gives goals, lesson outlines, and tools and handouts; the Classroom Practices outlines follow a launch-explore-summarize pattern, the Community Mathematics Exploration module includes samples of prospective teachers' work, and the case study module offers sample schedules for a term.

How these awards connect to this product

The website and the module files credit NSF award 1228034 (DRL). Award 1020155 has the same title and abstract and the same PI (Drake) at Iowa State University, starting in 2010 and ending in 2012; 1228034 started at Michigan State in 2011. The project's sources cite only 1228034, so 1020155 is listed as the same project's earlier record, not as a confirmed funder of the posted modules.
