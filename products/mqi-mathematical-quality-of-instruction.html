Mathematical Quality of Instruction (MQI) rubric and MQI Coaching

A math-specific classroom observation rubric with a video-based coaching model; coaches and PD leaders learn to use the rubric through Harvard's paid trainings or a request for its rater training.

Before you use it

  • We found no posted copy of the full rubric. CEPR's trainings teach it: the coach trainings cover the MQI rubric and how to use it as a coaching tool, and the rater training is a set of modules that teach you to rate classroom video with it. Per Harvard pages archived Nov-Dec 2025, the self-paced online course was $249 (dates TBD) and the Virtual Institute $999 per person, $899 each for groups of two or more, with partial scholarships; confirm current prices with CEPR. Districts can contact CEPR for a custom training. For scoring video in research, CEPR's rubric page links a request form for the rater training.
  • It is for math coaches, supervisors, PD leaders and teacher teams. CEPR recommends the coach training for educators using the MQI for professional learning and says the rater training fits researchers better. The online course frames it as non-evaluative feedback.
  • Plan time: the online course takes about ten hours over two weeks. The 2026 Virtual Institute was scheduled (per a Dec 2025 capture) for two-hour webinars twice a week from July 7 to July 30, with about an hour of pre-work before each, for 24 contact hours. Those dates have passed, and we found no later dates.
  • You'll need classroom video. The coaching cycles are video-based, and the rater training has you rate video of real lessons.
  • The rubric was built from U.S. elementary and middle school classrooms; the CEPR pages we saved don't mention high school.

What a teacher gets

The MQI rubric is an observation tool for the mathematics in a lesson rather than general pedagogy or classroom climate. Its codes fall in four domains: Common Core-aligned student practices (students asking questions, explaining, reasoning), working with students and mathematics (how the teacher interprets and responds to student ideas and errors), richness of the mathematics (explanations, connections, multiple methods, generalizations, mathematical language), and errors and imprecision. Heather Hill and colleagues at the University of Michigan and Harvard built it as a research instrument between 2003 and 2012, drawing on U.S. elementary and middle school classrooms.

MQI Coaching turns the rubric into a coaching model: video-based coaching cycles in which teachers reflect on their own recorded lessons, plus an MQI Video Library of classroom clips. Harvard's Center for Education Policy Research (CEPR) listed a $249 ten-hour online course in late 2025, a summer Virtual Institute for coaches, custom district trainings, and a separate set of rater-training modules with practice quizzes for those who want to score video.

Who made it

MQI Coaching, Center for Education Policy Research, Harvard University. Developed by Heather C. Hill.

How these awards connect to this product

CEPR says the MQI Coaching model was developed as part of an NSF-funded research study and lists that study's funders as NSF grants 1348144 and 0918383 and the Institute of Education Sciences (R305C090023). The 1348144 record describes developing and field-testing a coaching protocol centered on the MQI instrument. The link for 0918383 rests only on CEPR's funder list: its record describes a study of Math Solutions professional development, teacher knowledge and instruction and does not name the MQI. The link for 1221693 rests only on its own record, which describes trying different ways to deliver MQI-aligned professional development built on critiquing classroom video; no saved source lists it among CEPR's funders. No saved source says who paid for developing the MQI rubric itself (2003-2012, at the University of Michigan and Harvard), so we use "Funded part of this product": NSF is shown to have funded the coaching model, not the rubric. Awards 2100961, 2100793, 2100830 and 2317284 fund adding equity rubrics (EAR-MI) to MQI Coaching; they are left off because no saved source shows that work in the offerings described here.
