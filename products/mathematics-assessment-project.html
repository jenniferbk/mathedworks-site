Before you use it

  • Everything is free to download without a login. The lessons may be reproduced unmodified for noncommercial use (CC BY-NC-ND 3.0); the PD modules carry a CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 license.
  • Plan on two class periods and extra prep: the guide says the whole process takes at least two 55 to 60 minute class periods, that lesson prep "will take more time" than usual, and that most teachers at first need more time than suggested.
  • Print and cut ahead: the distance–time lesson we checked needs two copies of the pre-assessment per student, mini-whiteboards, and three cut-up card sets, paper and a glue stick for each group.
  • No lesson requires technology.
  • The materials date from 2013–2015 and cite the Common Core State Standards; MAP calls its prototype tests "still in draft and unpolished form."

What a teacher gets

The Mathematics Assessment Project (MAP) posts free assessment materials designed by the Shell Center team at the University of Nottingham with UC Berkeley. Its core is 100 Classroom Challenges, formative assessment lessons: 20 for each of grades 6, 7 and 8 and 40 for high school. Some develop concepts; others ask students to solve non-routine problems. Each lesson comes as an all-in-one PDF with a PowerPoint. In the lesson we checked, students first try a short task alone; the teacher reviews the work and writes questions, students then match cards in small groups, and they finish by improving their first answers.

MAP also offers summative tasks at three levels (Novice, Apprentice, Expert), each with a scoring rubric and pre-scored student work, prototype tests, and five draft professional development modules. One refereed journal article by researchers outside the project studies the lessons (listed below).

Who made it

Mathematics Assessment Project, Shell Center (University of Nottingham) with UC Berkeley; part of the Gates Foundation's Math Design Collaborative. Developed by Alan Schoenfeld, Hugh Burkhardt and Malcolm Swan.

Curriculum analysis.Compares four MAP Concept Development lessons with four lessons from the Israeli MesiMatica program for how they support teachers' formative assessment. The authors find MAP exposes teachers to a wide range of possible student responses and gives highly specified guidance for running the lesson itself.ZDM aims and scope: papers are by invitation, "followed by internal peer review by the guest-editors and external review by invited experts"; submission guidelines list single-blind peer review. Authors at the University of Haifa and the Weizmann Institute. "No funding was received for conducting this study." The competing-interests statement discloses that two authors led MesiMatica, the program compared with MAP; no tie to MAP, and no author appears on MAP's team page.

Each study is about this product, by researchers with no tie to its developer we could find.

Who funded it

NSF did not fund this product. It is listed because independent researchers studied it in peer-reviewed venues (see Research behind it). Funders named by the developer: Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. MAP's About page says the materials were produced by UC Berkeley and the Shell Center team at the University of Nottingham "with support from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation" and that development would not have been possible without that support.
