Before you use it

  • Cost per module (NCTM store, Oct 25, 2026): the facilitator package is $74.91 and includes access to the casebook content; the casebook, which NCTM calls the key resource for participants, is sold separately at $48.73 print or $34.11–$34.12 PDF. The package is sold only through NCTM's online catalog (no purchase orders), and digital purchases can't be returned.
  • You need a facilitator. The online guide is written for the person leading the seminar, who prepares each session from its checklist and readings. The materials don't say whether facilitators need training.
  • Technology: the guide is online and needs Internet and a computer or tablet; its in-class agenda embeds each session's video for showing to participants. The license is for one user, and logins may not be shared.
  • Prep and homework, from the Session 2 sample: a poster of the session's main idea, base-ten blocks, index cards, interlocking cubes and handouts to copy. Participants interview a student for homework; before the next session the facilitator reads their earlier written homework on analyzing student methods and writes a response to each participant.
  • Check availability before planning Making Meaning for Operations: NCTM's store listing showed facilitator packages for the other six modules but not that one. The Building a System of Tens package dates from 2009 and its print casebook from 2016; a 2015 revision added Common Core connections.

What a teacher gets

Developing Mathematical Ideas is a professional development curriculum in seven modules: Building a System of Tens, Making Meaning for Operations, Reasoning Algebraically About Operations, Examining Features of Shape, Measuring Space in One, Two, and Three Dimensions, Modeling with Data, and Patterns, Functions, and Change. Each module has a casebook of 28 to 34 teacher-written classroom cases and an online facilitator's guide with videos. The Building a System of Tens casebook spans kindergarten to grade 8 and ends with a research essay. A seminar runs eight three-hour sessions.

For each session the facilitator's guide gives an overview, a preparation checklist, mathematics background notes, a timed agenda (also as a Word file) and "Maxine's Journal," a fictional facilitator's account of the session. In the free Session 2 sample from Building a System of Tens, teachers set group norms, work a math activity on multiplying by 10, watch interviews with three students, discuss chapter 2's cases in grade-level groups, and get homework to interview a student. The aim is teachers' understanding of the content and of student thinking.

How these awards connect to this product

0242609 (Teaching to the Big Ideas of Early Algebra) is the only listed award whose record names DMI: it designs two DMI modules on generalization and functions, says five modules on arithmetic, geometry and data already existed, and says the algebra modules would complete the series. Matching those two modules to today's Reasoning Algebraically About Operations and Patterns, Functions, and Change is our inference. 9731064, with the same three PIs, creates case-based K-8 professional development materials on geometry, measurement and data analysis and builds on a prior Teaching to the Big Ideas project on number; its record does not use the name DMI. No source says the current NCTM edition or the 2015 revision was NSF-funded, and the NCTM store pages do not credit NSF. Not listed: 9254393 (Teaching To The Big Ideas, 1993), whose record does not name DMI; Schifter's note says the casebooks grew from a project with thirty-six teachers, which matches that award, but the connection is our inference. Also not listed: 1019769, a TERC efficacy study of DMI rather than funding for the materials; this card cites no findings from it.
