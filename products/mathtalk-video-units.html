Before you use it

  • The videos play on the project site with no account. No price is shown, the footer carries only "© 2026 MathTalk", and we found no reuse license.
  • You need internet access and a screen to show or assign the episodes; the one episode page we read (parabolas lesson 1, episode 1) offers a captions option; we did not check other episodes. The PD decks are Google Drive links, and the developers say to download them as PowerPoint, because opening them in Google Slides may lose formatting and content.
  • Unit sizes vary: the home page puts each unit at 40 videos, but the parabolas menu lists 10 lessons and 57 episodes. Episode lengths are not listed.
  • According to the PD materials, their clips show students in grades 8, 9 and 11; we did not check grade levels for every unit. The covariational reasoning unit features two undergraduates, Zoe and Gisele, and ends with building the sine function.
  • The covariational reasoning unit has no teacher page. A project team member wrote teacher support materials for five of the units.

What a teacher gets

Project MathTalk films pairs of students working through a sequence of tasks with a teacher, then edits the unscripted footage into short episodes with annotations and animated summaries. The student section has eight units: parabolas, proportional reasoning, algebraic expressions, exponential functions, logarithms, multiplying binomials, trigonometric concepts and covariational reasoning. Episodes are grouped into lessons; the parabolas unit, for example, has 10 lessons that run from building a parabola out of its focus-and-directrix definition to deriving the vertex form.

The teacher section covers seven of the units. The two teacher lesson pages we read (parabolas and proportions, lesson 1) list the Common Core standards involved and commentary tied to time stamps in the episodes; the parabolas page also lists targeted understandings. The developers suggest assigning episodes as homework, using one to launch a class discussion, or using a set of lessons in place of a unit. A teacher-educator section offers four PowerPoint decks for secondary teacher professional development, each built around MathTalk clips and meant for one or two sessions.

In the NSF API, 1416789 (Lobato, San Diego State, 2014-2018) created and studied online videos for two mathematics units, and 1907782 (Lobato, 2019-2024) developed and studied unscripted mathematics videos.
