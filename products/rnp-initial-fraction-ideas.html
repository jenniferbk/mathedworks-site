Rational Number Project: Initial Fraction Ideas

A free 23-lesson fraction unit for grades 4–6 built on fraction circles, counters and paper folding, with a teacher's guide, quizzes and student interviews.

Before you use it

  • The University of Minnesota pages that hosted the lessons return "not found" (checked Oct 25, 2026); download the PDFs from the archived copy linked here.
  • The Flash virtual manipulatives listed on the module page won't run: Adobe ended Flash Player support at the end of 2020. Lesson 1's wrap-up uses a transparency; other lessons' materials were not checked.
  • You make the fraction circles: copy the masters onto index stock in the colors noted on each master, then cut them out. The developers suggest enlisting students, parents or aides; you'll also need two-sided counters (about 30 per student) and paper strips.
  • There are no formal algorithms; plan to follow with the companion module or your own instruction for symbolic procedures. Lesson time is not fixed: the developers say one day is often enough, but a lesson is not always one day's work.
  • Materials date from 1997 (revised 2009). The teacher's guide cites NCTM's 2000 Principles and Standards and does not mention the Common Core.

What a teacher gets

Initial Fraction Ideas is the Rational Number Project's fraction unit for upper elementary grades, first published in 1997 as Fraction Lessons for the Middle Grades and revised in 2009. Its 23 lessons build meaning for fractions as parts of a whole, informal ways to order fractions, equivalence, and adding and subtracting with concrete models. It does not teach formal algorithms; the companion module, Fraction Operations and Initial Decimal Ideas, picks up there.

The teacher's guide describes the lesson format: a 5–10 minute warm-up reviewing earlier lessons, then a whole-class introduction, partner or small-group work with manipulatives, and a wrap-up. A "Comments" column beside the teaching steps explains student thinking and common misunderstandings documented in the RNP's research. The appendix has fraction circle masters, a parent and child activity, four quizzes, a written test and four student interviews. The developers wrote it for grades 4–6 and note use in remedial settings with older students.

How these awards connect to this product

The RNP credits NSF support for the project as a whole from 1979 to 2002. The teacher's guide says Merlyn Behr wrote the first draft of the fraction materials for the project's initial research with children; the earliest listed awards, both to Behr, are on manipulatives and rational number. The teacher's guide does not say which award funded the published lessons, and the later awards listed here are not shown to have funded them directly. The RNP site lists its 1995 award as 9408915; in the NSF API that number is an unrelated economics award, and 9454371 matches the site's title, PI and $1,904,888 amount, so we infer it is the correct number. MDR 9144969, listed on the site with 8955346, was not found in the NSF API.
