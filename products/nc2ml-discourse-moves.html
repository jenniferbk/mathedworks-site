Before you use it

  • The kits, brief and posters download as PDFs and a zip file with no login, and no price is posted. The PD slide decks and talk bubbles are Google Slides: you can view them without signing in, but the "make a copy" links ask you to sign in to a Google account.
  • These are supports for classroom talk, not lessons or tasks. Plan to use the moves with your own curriculum; the project says they work with any curricular materials.
  • Written by and for North Carolina high school teachers: the Revoicing administrator kit we read ties that move to NC teaching standards and the NC Portrait of a Graduate, and the brief links the moves to the NC Mathematical Practice Standards. The examples we read use high school algebra (factoring with algebra tiles, a doubling-pennies problem).
  • No session lengths are given in the deck we read; it says sessions can be adapted or chunked into smaller sessions for your school or district.
  • The kits were released in 2023–24 (the ones we read were last updated between August 2023 and February 2024) and are marked as still being co-designed, with a feedback form; check nc2ml.org for newer versions.

What a teacher gets

NC2ML, a North Carolina collaborative, built resources around nine discourse moves for high school math: encouraging in-progress thinking, revoicing, inviting participation, waiting, assessing and advancing student thinking, assigning competence, orienting students to others' work, and attributing students' ideas. The project plan says the moves fit any curriculum and any class setting, and runs them as a monthly campaign, one move a month.

Each move has a Teaching and Learning Kit describing the move, why it matters, what teachers and students do, and an example in action; the two kits we read add sentence starters, tips and questions to consider with colleagues. A Professional Development Kit of slides for PLCs or PD has teachers experience the move in a math task, explore it through vignettes and plan next steps; the deck we read (Inviting Participation) has notes for coaches. The page says each Administrative Leadership Kit connects its move to broader strategies such as the Portrait of a Graduate; the one we read (Revoicing) also cites NC teaching standards. Extras include a research brief, sentence-starter posters and editable talk bubbles.

How these awards connect to this product

The Teaching and Learning kits, the Administrative Leadership kit and the one-page project plan we read each print the same line, "This work was supported by the National Science Foundation" under DRL-2100895 (East Carolina), DRL-2100903 (UNC Greensboro), DRL-2100947 (UNC Charlotte) and DRL-2100833 (Appalachian State), and add that prior related work was supported by the NC Department of Public Instruction. The four NSF records are one collaborative project, "Co-Designing for Statewide Alignment of a Vision for High Quality Mathematics Instruction," whose abstract says partners will develop instructional frameworks and research and practice briefs. The research-to-practice brief and the Inviting Participation PD slide deck carry no NSF line; the brief's footer names NC2ML, the UNCG School of Education and the NC Department of Public Instruction, and each move's webinar recording is labeled "DPI Webinar Recording." Because the sources tie NSF to some components and not others, we use "Funded part of this product."
