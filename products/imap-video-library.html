What a teacher gets

The IMAP Searchable Video Library is a collection of video clips, images and related resources showing how K-5 children reason about and solve mathematics problems. Pearson describes it as built for professional development facilitators and math coaches, to help teachers see how children think mathematically, deepen their own mathematics, and consider how to support students' understanding. Pearson lists the San Diego State University Foundation as author. A San Diego State page says IMAP, a federally funded project there, was designed to bring children's mathematical thinking into content courses for prospective elementary teachers, and that the first six clips of a separate 12-clip set came from it; we infer this library comes from the same project.

The current version is a digital product published by Pearson in 2020 (copyright 2021), sold as Instant Access. We could not examine the library, so we cannot say how the search works, which topics and grades the clips cover, or what facilitator materials come with it.

How these awards connect to this product

Pearson lists the library's author as the San Diego State University Foundation, the awardee on NSF award 9979902, titled Integrating Mathematics and Pedagogy (IMAP), to Philipp with Sowder as co-PI. A San Diego State University page describes IMAP as a federally funded three-year project led by Philipp and says the first six clips of a separate 12-clip set came from it; it does not name NSF. The award record runs five years, describes studies of early field experiences and content courses for prospective teachers, and does not mention video. No source ties the 229-clip library to this award, so the connection rests on the matching project name, PI and institution and is recorded as inferred. Philipp's later award 0455785 (teacher needs during sustained professional development) is not listed because its record does not mention IMAP or the clips.
