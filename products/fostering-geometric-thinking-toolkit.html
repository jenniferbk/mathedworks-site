Before you use it

  • Only institutions can buy it: Heinemann marks it a restricted product, sold to buyers ordering for an educational institution, so an individual teacher can't order it alone. It lists at $351.33, or $263.50 at the web/school price (checked Oct 25, 2026).
  • This is PD for a facilitator to run with a group of six to twenty teachers of grades 5–10, not classroom lessons. You don't need to be a geometry expert, but the authors look for some experience leading study groups or professional development, or a willingness to learn.
  • Plan the year: ten four-hour monthly meetings or twenty two-hour biweekly ones. Schedule each Analyze Student Work session at least two weeks after its Do Math session so teachers can try the problem with their students.
  • You'll need equipment to show video and a computer that reads the DVD-ROM of handouts, videos and applets. Necessary supplies include patty paper, string, straws, straightedges, scissors and sticky notes in four colors; Geometer's Sketchpad and computer access are listed as very helpful, not required.
  • Published in 2008.

What a teacher gets

The Fostering Geometric Thinking Toolkit is a facilitator's guide for a year-long teacher study group on geometric thinking, written by Mark Driscoll and colleagues, copyrighted by Education Development Center (EDC) and published by Heinemann in 2008. It is meant for groups of six to twenty teachers of grades 5 through 10. Twenty two-hour sessions make up forty hours, covering geometric properties, transformations and measurement through a framework of geometric habits of mind.

Session 1 combines an introduction with Do Math; after that, sessions alternate. In each even-numbered Do Math session, teachers work a geometry problem together (folding paper, dissecting shapes, dilating figures, finding centers of rotation, tangrams and others). Between sessions, they try the problem with their own students and collect student work. The following odd-numbered session analyzes that work. Session 20 concludes the course with a last Do Math. The book gives facilitation notes for every session, guidance on leading discussions, and start-up letters and forms. A DVD-ROM holds the handouts, video of students working the problems, sample student work, computer applets and research slideshows.

How these awards connect to this product

The Toolkit's copyright page states the work is supported under NSF Grant No. ESI-0353409, the EDC award to develop a geometry professional development curriculum for grades 5–9. Award 0821950 funded a later efficacy study of the Toolkit with teachers of English language learners; its record describes research on the Toolkit, not its development.
