What didn't make it, and why

Most of what we left out failed one of three tests: we couldn't confirm an NSF award behind it, we couldn't find it for sale or download today, or a teacher couldn't use it without the research team. A few products were outside K–12 or written for people who train teachers rather than for teachers. Some well-loved materials are on this page for those reasons, not because anyone judged them weak.

Exclusions came at two stages. Six products were drafted as full listings and then rejected after checking, and one is on hold. Earlier, while building the shortlist, the two shortlist agents and the adjudicator set aside groups and products before any listing was written, including six candidates from a later search of PME-NA conference proceedings. Both stages are below, sorted by reason. "Agent A," "Agent B" and "the adjudicator" refer to the three agents described in How we chose.

In October 2026 we learned we had missed Amy Ellis and Rob Ely's Playful Mathematics activities, which are now listed. Our agents had checked one project page and stopped. We then re-checked 18 of these exclusions, searching every NSF award, the open web and web.archive.org. Seven came back. Project AIM's Activating Math Talk, Project MATHEMATICS!, COMAP's Mathematics: Modeling Our World, the PCMI Teacher Program books, Young Mathematicians and NCTM's Problems of the Week are now listed. The National Library of Virtual Manipulatives is still being checked. The reasons below for the rest come from that re-check.

If one of these products does have an NSF award, or is available somewhere we missed, email us at mathedworks@gmail.com and we'll check it again.

Why weren't these listed? No NSF award could be tied to the product

We list only products that trace to a confirmed NSF award. These failed that test.

Drafted, then rejected:

  • Talk Moves, Third Edition (Chapin, O'Connor, Anderson). The publisher credits a U.S. Department of Education project. The candidate NSF award (0231893) is a grade 7 discourse study by two of the authors, and no source ties it to this K–6 book.
  • Implementing Standards-Based Mathematics Instruction: A Casebook for Professional Development, 2nd edition. ERIC credits the Ford Foundation for the 2000 first edition, whose cases came from the QUASAR project. ERIC lists no sponsor for the 2009 second edition, and no publisher page mentions NSF. The authors also held an NSF award (COMET, 9731428), but no source connects it to this book. If the second edition's acknowledgments credit an NSF award, the card will be reopened.
  • Thinking Mathematically: Integrating Arithmetic and Algebra in Elementary School. ERIC lists no sponsor for the book, and credits the authors' 2000 research report on the same work to the U.S. Office of Educational Research and Improvement. NSF award 9911679 describes a book plus video CDs for K–6 teachers, which matches the book's format, but no source connects them. If the book credits NSF, the card will be reopened.
  • Lesson Study "Conduct a Cycle" resources (lessonresearch.net). The only NSF link is a logo among other funders' logos, with no award number. The site's Fractions Toolkit credits the Institute of Education Sciences (IES).

Set aside while building the shortlist:

  • 5 Practices for Orchestrating Productive Mathematics Discussions (Smith and Stein), by the adjudicator. No NSF acknowledgment turned up in the book listings, the metadata of the 2008 article behind it, or the group's award abstracts. The book's own acknowledgments couldn't be checked. The same group's NSF-funded COMET casebook is listed.
  • John Mason's materials, by Agent A. The only NSF award matching his name belongs to a different person.
  • Bridges in Mathematics (Math Learning Center), by Agent A. The product is live, but no NSF award was found.
  • Illustrative Mathematics, Desmos, GeoGebra, youcubed and the Mathematics Assessment Project, by Agent B. These products are widely used, but no NSF development award was found. Agent B noted that Illustrative Mathematics and the Mathematics Assessment Project are Gates-funded and that Desmos and GeoGebra are commercial or European. Agent A separately found that the one related NSF award (1502778) funds teacher-knowledge items and tasks, not the Illustrative Mathematics curriculum.
  • ALEKS, ST Math and Wuzzit Trouble, by Agent B. No NSF award was found for any of them.
  • Agile Mind / Intensified Algebra, by Agent B. The only award found supports a Dana Center Algebra II project, not this product.
  • Shodor Interactivate, by Agent B. The site is live, but Shodor's NSF awards fund computational-science institutes, and none was found for Interactivate itself.
  • AlgebraByExample, Contrasting Cases, Mathtutor and Learning Mathematics through Representations, by Agent B. Each is funded by IES or the U.S. Department of Education, and no NSF award was found.
  • Graspable Math / From Here to There, by Agent B. Both products are IES-funded. The developer's NSF-funded teacher dashboard is still a prototype.
  • Model-eliciting activities (Lesh, Doerr), by Agent B. No NSF award is clearly tied to a maintained activity library.
  • Teacher Education by Design (Kazemi, Lampert, Franke), by Agent B. The site is live, but a second search found no NSF award tied to it.

Why weren't these listed? We couldn't find them available today

A product has to be obtainable now, free or paid. These have confirmed NSF awards, but we found no current source.

  • Mathematics in Context (Romberg), by both agents and the adjudicator. NSF awards are confirmed, but Britannica's site for it has been empty since 2019, and only used copies turned up.
  • SimCalc MathWorlds (Kaput, Roschelle, Hegedus), by both agents and the adjudicator. The NSF history is strong, but the Kaput Center site doesn't resolve. The only live remnant is a calculator app page in Texas Instruments' discontinued-software section, and the units need software that is no longer distributed.
  • Fathom Dynamic Data, by the adjudicator. Its site says it is no longer supported and points users to CODAP, which is listed.
  • National Library of Virtual Manipulatives, by Agent B. The site timed out for us again in October 2026, though an archived copy from early October shows it running its applets in the browser. A person is checking whether it loads.
  • Middle Grades MATH Thematics and SIMMS, by Agent B. A second search found no current seller, only library copies.
  • CME Project, by Agent B. No live publisher page was found.
  • The Algebra Project, by both agents. Archived copies show a free curriculum portal through at least 2023, but the site now blocks automated access and its homepage no longer links the portal. A person is checking it.
  • Big Math for Little Kids, by Agent A. A second search found no current publisher, only used copies.
  • M-DISC (Mathematics Discourse in Secondary Classrooms), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. NSF award 0918117 names the curriculum, but the publisher's store link now redirects to a generic notice and the companion site is gone.
  • COMPS / COMMIT-A word-problem tutor (Xin, Purdue), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. The project site credits NSF but offers no way to sign up, download or buy the tutor.
  • TalkMoves app (Sumner, Jacobs), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. Sign-up requires a registration code, and nothing public says how a teacher gets one.

Four products first excluded for availability were later found and listed. Both agents had excluded the Interactive Mathematics Program, and the adjudicator found its publisher. Agent A had excluded Math-Mapper 6-8 and Contexts for Learning Mathematics, which the adjudicator found, and the IMAP video library, which Agent B kept.

Why weren't these listed? They fall outside what we cover

We cover K–12 math products that a teacher can use without the research team. We don't cover research instruments that need trained scorers, or research programs that left no product.

Drafted, then rejected:

  • Building Blocks PreK Math. The only edition on sale is McGraw Hill's 2025 PreK program for three- and four-year-olds. NSF's original award covered PreK to grade 2, but we found no K–2 edition or component on sale today. The same team's Learning Trajectories site is listed.

Set aside while building the shortlist:

  • Zoombinis (TERC, FableVision), by the adjudicator. Its NSF award studied computational thinking with an existing commercial game, and the game teaches logic rather than mathematics.
  • Math Teachers' Circles (American Institute of Mathematics), by the adjudicator. Its NSF award studied an existing program rather than developing teacher materials, and its main site didn't respond.
  • Amy Hackenberg's research, by both agents and the adjudicator, confirmed by the re-check. Her NSF CAREER award promised differentiation materials and an assessment, but neither was released. What exists for teachers is one paywalled practitioner article and some conference handouts. Her co-authored book Developing Fractions Knowledge is being considered separately.
  • Data Modeling (Lehrer and Schauble), by both agents. The NSF research is strong, but the units' website domain has expired, and the units sat behind a login even when it was live.
  • Nicole McNeil's research, by Agent B. Her NSF award produced a number-sense assessment that is posted openly, but as a research measure with no teacher guide. A teacher version is funded under a 2026 award and isn't out yet.
  • Cobb's MIST project, by Agent A. Its outputs are district-improvement research, not a teacher product.
  • Project Aspire's teacher-knowledge instrument (Thompson), by Agent A and the adjudicator. Its rubrics require trained scoring. The same group's free DIRACC calculus text is listed.
  • Mathematical Quality of Instruction (Hill), by Agent A, and Kersting's Classroom Video Analysis, by Agent B. Both need trained raters or scoring.
  • Math Habits Tool (Melhuish, Heaton; Teachers Development Group), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. It is a classroom observation instrument for coaches, principals and researchers, not a teaching product, and its public pages don't credit NSF.
  • MCLE exemplar lessons (Dietiker), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. They are six lesson folders in a shared drive from a research study, too thin and unpackaged to stand beside the rest of the list.
  • MS-GGU graphing tasks (Lee, Paoletti), by the adjudicator after the PME-NA search. Three tasks from a study that runs until 2027. They're on a watch list.

Why weren't these listed? They're written for teacher educators, not teachers

We list materials for practicing K–12 teachers. These products are built for university courses that prepare new teachers.

  • TEACH Math learning modules, drafted, then rejected. The modules are written for prospective PreK–8 teachers in university methods courses, and assume a practicum classroom, a child to interview and course write-ups. Practicing teachers can't use them as they stand.
  • LessonSketch (Herbst, Chazan), by Agent B and the adjudicator. The platform is built for teacher educators, and its site returned a bot challenge. Agent A had listed it.
  • Preparing to Teach Mathematics with Technology, by the adjudicator. Its audience is teacher educators and prospective teachers, and the award number it credits returns no record from NSF. The same group's InSTEP and ESTEEM materials are listed.

Which product is on hold?

  • ModelME Dream2B, a game-based fractions curriculum from Jessica Hunt's group. An early draft rejected it on the belief that teachers couldn't register. The verifier then found a teacher registration form. Whether registration works, and how student IDs are issued, can only be checked by a person signing up, which is queued. If it works, the card will be finished and listed. The same group's FAACT fraction curriculum is listed.

Suggested by teachers, but we couldn't find independent research

Teachers suggest products they like, and we check each one against the bar for products without NSF funding: peer-reviewed research by people with no tie to the developer. These were suggested, and teachers spoke well of them, but we found no such research. That doesn't make them weak. It means we can't yet say they are research-based.

  • Math Medic (high school, Algebra 1 through AP Calculus), suggested by a teacher in a UGA teacher-education class in October 2026. ERIC, Crossref, OpenAlex and NSF's award database turned up no studies of it. Its lessons are free with a free account at mathmedic.com.

If you know of a peer-reviewed study of one of these, email mathedworks@gmail.com and we'll check it.
