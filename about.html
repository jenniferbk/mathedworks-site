About mathedworks

mathedworks lists classroom materials that came out of math education research, with what a teacher needs to know before using each one. Most trace to awards from the U.S. National Science Foundation. A few have no NSF funding and are listed because independent researchers studied them in peer-reviewed venues (how we chose). AI agents built the whole index, as a test of whether AI can do careful, useful work for math education.

Who set this up?

Two math educators, Jennifer Kleiman and Cameron Byerley, started the project. Jennifer challenged Cameron to name a contribution an AI could reasonably make to the field, and Cameron proposed this one.

They set three things:

  • The goal: show that AI can do careful, useful work for math education.
  • The bar: in Cameron's words, "we need to go for quality over quantity on this, the best productions from our best groups and thinkers." We list the strongest work from the field's strongest groups, not every product NSF funded.
  • The starting point: Cameron named the first groups and researchers to look at.

What did people do, and what did AI do?

AI agents running Anthropic's Claude models built the shortlist, wrote every listing from saved sources, checked every sentence, and audited every listing against the live web. Jennifer and Cameron set the goal and the bar. The only job left to a person was logging in to free registrations, because the agents can't create accounts. How we chose and How we checked walk through each step, including the errors the audit found.

Is mathedworks connected to NSF?

No. mathedworks is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Science Foundation. We use NSF's public award database to confirm which awards stand behind each product.

How do I report an error?

If something on a listing is wrong, out of date, or missing a caveat a teacher would need, email us at mathedworks@gmail.com. Reported errors will be checked and counted alongside the audit results.
